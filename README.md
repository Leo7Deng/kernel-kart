# Kernel Kart

One-button mascot kart racing in the spirit of *Formula Cartoon All-Stars*. Plain JS modules on a canvas, built with Vite.

**Play it on the web:** https://leo7deng.github.io/kernel-kart/ (single races, cups, time trials; online rooms still need someone running the game's server, below). `npm run publish:web` updates it.

**Run it:** `npm install`, then `npm run play` (or `npm run dev`) and open the address it prints. `play` serves a clean copy of the last commit and moves to each new one as it lands, so a reload never catches a change half-made. `npm run build` puts a hostable copy in `dist/`. `npm test` replays every track headless and checks nothing has changed. A "What's new" button on the menu's title screen (or `C` on the menu; also on the results screen) opens a changelog built from git history (one version per day of commits, newest first; the version is `<major>.<day>.<commits that day>`: 1.0 started on 2026-10-18 with the batch of new maps and items, and the days count up from there), so it needs no manual upkeep. It lists only changes to the game (behind-the-scenes ones like docs and tests are left out), each tagged with its kind (Maps, Balance, Items, Racers, Racing, Online, Menu & HUD, Look & sound, Fixes), and the chips along the top filter the list. A red badge on it counts the changes since you last opened it, and those are marked "new" in the list. Beside it, **Settings** has the **Style**, for the menus, the race's HUD, the item icons and boxes and the items' effects (the tracks, cars and mascots look the same in all of them; it switches at once): **Classic**, the glossy, glowing look; **Classic Flat**, the same layout, colours and fonts with the gradients, glows, shine and rainbows taken out (solid fills in Classic's indigo, red-orange and gold, simple soft shadows, a gentle two-tone at most, the inked and extruded titles kept); **Racing**, like a race game's menus: black and carbon panels, racing red and signal yellow, heavy italic condensed capitals, skewed number plates, red and yellow stripes, chevrons and a chequered strip here and there, number plates for the places; and **Premium**, warm and polished: espresso panels with a faint warm sheen, an amber and copper accent, warm off-white type in Montserrat and Manrope, soft shadows and generous rounded corners. None of them has glows or rainbows except Classic. **Text size** (Normal, Large, Extra large) makes the words in the menus, the results, the online room and the pause card bigger, and the race's HUD with them (on a phone only as far as it still fits), for a small phone or a TV across the room. Then music and sound-effects volume, mute (in step with `Backspace`), screen shake, flashes, reduced effects (fewer particles, same race), a Show FPS counter and **Drift** (on by default: the third key to drift, see How it plays; off, the drift key, its hints and the touch button are gone and the game is the classic two-button one) and **3D models (experimental)** (off by default: every racer rides as a cartoon 3D model that turns with its kart, so you see its side, back or face depending on where it's driving) and **Map cover art (experimental)** (off by default: the track cards on the map screen and the cups' little thumbnails show a picture of each map, the crab looming over the beach, the volcano erupting, the train at the crossing, instead of its layout; the big preview still shows the layout) and **3D cars (experimental)** (off by default, separate so you can judge cars and riders apart: every racer's car as a cartoon 3D car in its own paint, number and stripes (Gopher's red kart, Octo's white kart in its Git livery, Ferris's monster truck, Pyth's striped sports coupé, Tux's bobsled, Duke's formula car, Docky's lorry and its container, Swifty's dune buggy, Cpp's hypercar and Bugdroid's electric kart), seen from a little to the south so you see their tops and the side facing you, front wheels (the bobsled's front runners) steering and brake lights on, still exactly their size on the road; a racer two players share is in each one's colour, as in 2D); they are saved in the browser and `Esc` closes the panel.

**The menu** is a few screens, one after another. It opens with a short loading screen (the logo and a kart riding a bar, a second or two the first time, about half a second after that: the track pictures are kept in the browser between visits; any key or a tap skips it) that fades into **the title**: the logo and the four ways to play as big tiles, **Race** (one race, 1 to 4 players and bots), **Cup**, **Time trial** and **Online**, with **Race again** under them (or `Enter`): straight into the last setup. How to play, Settings, In the works and What's new sit in the corner. **Race**, **Cup** and **Time trial** go on to **Who's racing**: how many players, how many bots and their skill on a bar at the top, then a big slot for each player in their colour (P1 red, P2 blue, P3 green, P4 amber) with their racer turning slowly on a turntable (the 3D model riding its kart), its stats, and READY once they've picked; every racer is below to pick from, each carrying the tags of the players who have it (players may share one). Each player picks with their own keys, shown on their slot: gas for the next racer, drift for the one before, item for ready (with everyone ready, item again goes on); or tap a slot and then a racer. **The map** comes next: the picked track big on the left with what it's about, **How it plays** (the full text of its mechanics, the **Front-runner** meter and a Watch how clip where there is one), the laps and Drift; every track on the right (**Random** first: a different track each time you press START, while Rematch keeps the one it picked), with chips to show one ready-made cup's tracks or the **Recent** ones (the last 8 tracks you raced, offline or online, newest first), a **search box** that narrows the tracks to the ones whose name has what you type (`/` jumps to it; `Enter` or `↓` goes to the first match, `Esc` empties it) and a **Front-runner ratings** switch that puts every track's rating on its card at once (and can sort them, highest or lowest first). In a cup the right side has the ready-made cups and **Your own cup**; in a time trial the left side has the track's records, rivals and Share my ghost. **START** is at the bottom right, with your racers at the bottom left (click them to go back and change them). **Online** goes to its own screen: your name, Host a room or Join with a code. **Back** (top left) or `Esc` goes back a screen; the steps at the top (Racers, Track) jump between the two. Coming back from a race the menu opens on the map, so another race is one press: `Enter`. Everything works by keyboard: `Tab` moves between the parts of a screen, the arrow keys move across the racers and the tracks, `Enter` or `Space` picks, `Enter` on what's already picked goes on (Next, START); `H` opens How to play (driving and controls, the items and the item odds chart, as tabs), `S` Settings, `C` What's new. Everything is sized for a thumb (44 px or more), the text follows the browser's text size, and on a phone each screen fits: on its side the racers sit beside their slots and the map's text folds under How it plays. Everything you choose is remembered in the browser for next time: the mode, players and their racers, the track, your own cup or the ready-made one you chose, the time trial's track (kept apart from the race track), laps, bots and skill, the Front-runner switch and its order. Online it remembers your name and your racer (picked for you when you host or join, even if someone in the room has it too), and as a host the room you last ran (track, laps, bots, skill, single race or cup), which your next room opens with.

## How it plays
- **Hold gas** to accelerate; the car steers itself around the track.
- **Rocket start.** The gas already counts during the countdown. Start holding it as the last light flickers green (the last third of a second before GO, or right on GO) and you launch on a short boost with flame out of the exhaust. Hold it any earlier and you burn out: the wheels spin in a cloud of smoke and you're slow away. Pressing just after GO is a plain start. Boosts of every kind (pads, tricks, the rocket start, Turbo, a drift's mini-turbo) all but jump you up to their speed from cruising speed. While you hold it your kart revs on the grid (it shakes and puffs exhaust), so you can see the gas is held. The head start is the same for a quick launcher as for a slow one. Bots try it too, and the better ones get it right more often.
- The car follows one smooth line, no lane switching. It always wants the inside of a corner, but only as far in as the arc it can actually make at your speed.
- **Lift early and the line tightens** onto the apex: a shorter way round. Letting go also gives extra grip straight away, so lifting mid-corner pulls you in.
- **Arrive hot and it stays out in the middle.** Too fast for even that and the car just drives the widest arc it can, which carries it off the road. No sliding, no spin: you simply miss the corner.
- The run-off (grass/sand) drops everyone to the same crawl and the car steers back onto the nearest edge of the tarmac. It's only so wide, so you can't cut across it to another part of the track.
- Item boxes and nearby cars nudge the line a little: it eases toward a box when your hands are free and to one side of a slower car ahead.
- Cars are solid: rear-end someone and you both feel it; side contact shoves the lighter car. Everyone takes slightly different lines and moves over to pass, so the pack spreads out.
- **Drafting** behind another car gives you extra speed, so the pack stays close.
- **Drift** (on by default; turn it off in **Settings** for the classic two-button game; a third key: `E`, or `Shift` alone; `[` for P2 with two players, `N` and `\` for P2 and P3 with three, `B`, `,` and `[` for P2-P4 with four; a DRIFT button on touch). Hold it and the kart drifts toward the inside of the next corner: the rear steps out, it grips half as hard again, and its line tucks in further than its grip alone would take it, so you can carry speed through a hairpin or tuck in onto an inside box or bit. On a straight it moves your line over to the side of the next corner instead (aim for a box or a bit there), but it's no steering wheel: it only ever goes that way. It costs speed: a little in a corner, more on a straight (down to 90% of top speed). Held too long near top speed it strains (the kart shudders) and spins you out. Drift through a corner and the sparks off the rear wheels go blue (0.4 s), orange (0.85 s), then purple (1.4 s); let go for a mini-turbo, bigger each colour (+12/17/22% top speed for 0.5/0.8/1.1 s and a kick). Running off the road, a hit or a jump loses the charge. Bots drift too (with drift on): they pick the slow corners long enough to charge a spark, brake for the drift's extra grip, press it just before the corner and let go as it straightens out. Better bots drift more of them and time it well (Ruthless bots nearly every one, hanging on for the next colour when it's a moment away, Normal ones about half); Chill ones drift a few, let go late or early, and now and then hold it too long and spin. They leave it alone near a track's hazards, forks, ramps and crossings. A player who drifts every corner well still gains on a Normal bot. A mini-turbo counts as a boost for the secret shortcuts, so drifting out wide onto one on a purple turbo takes it.

## Ramps, tricks and cannons
A **ramp** (yellow and black lip) throws you into the air. How far depends on your speed, and in the air you can't steer or change speed: you land where you were pointing, so a ramp taken mid-corner lands you in the grass.
**Trick**: let go of the gas right at the lip (within a quarter of a second) and the kart spins in the air and gets a boost on landing. The lift costs a little speed on the way in, so it's a trade: entry speed for exit speed.
A ramp before a **crevasse** is a **cannon**: a fixed flight to the far side, but only if you hit the lip above about 80% of your kart's top speed. Slower and you drop in, lose everything and restart from well before the ramp. Tricking a cannon is the gamble: the lift can leave you under the gate. Bots floor it for cannons and trick ramps about as often as they're good.

## Secret shortcuts
Some tracks hide a secret far out in the run-off on the outside of a corner, where the steering never takes you: look for something glowing out there. Running over it does nothing (it only stirs), unless you come in **blazing**: on a boost (a Turbo, an Overclock, a boost pad, or one that ran out a moment ago), or, depending on the secret, phased out by a Ghost or sliding on a Memory Leak's oil. Then it takes you (any part of your kart touching it is enough), and puts you back on the road further up the lap at a set speed. Some sit past the end of the run-off, where only a blazing kart can carry on out to them. Nothing can hit you on the way. It's a gamble, not a free skip: the only way out there is to fire your boost into the corner instead of down a straight and keep your foot in while the run-off bleeds your speed, and fired a little early or late you just end up on the grass. Hit it and it saves a second or two. Good bots go for it now and then.

## Bits
Little glowing chips lie on the road in short lines: tucked in at the apex of the slow corners (lift early and the line tightens onto them), at every other slow corner far out on the exit instead (stay on the gas through it and you run wide onto them, at the risk of the run-off: is it worth it?), out on the exit of a few fast sweepers (carry speed and you run wide over them, or off the road if you overdo it) and down the middle of the longest straight. Each one you hold adds 0.4% to your top speed, up to **10** (a badge on your card shows how many; it goes gold when you're full, and a full kart leaves them for the others). Any hit (a spin-out, an item, a melt-down, a moon, a squish) throws **3** of yours out behind you, for whoever comes past; you can't take your own back for a couple of seconds. A bit comes back 6 s after it's taken; thrown ones fade after 10 s. Bots lean toward a bit near their line, the better ones more.

## Items
Three or more rows of **?** boxes per lap (more on a long lap), mostly two side by side; drive through one to grab an item. About a third of the rows are a single box instead, picked track by track and set in a corner, where your line depends on your throttle: one on the outside (out wide on a corner's exit, or round a carousel's outer wall) is for a kart that carries its speed through, one on the inside of a fast corner is for a kart that lifts a touch and tucks in. The start straight, the rows where roads meet again and the rows before a big hazard stay pairs. On a track with more than one road (a new route every lap, or a split), a row on a stretch only one road uses is a single box down the middle; rows where roads share the stretch keep their pair. On a few tracks one row is a single box out wide on the exit of a fast corner instead: carry speed out of the corner to run wide through it, or lift and tuck in past it. You can hold **two**: the one you'll fire next (shown big, the moment you grab it) and one in reserve (the small badge), which moves up when you fire. You only take one box at a time (the closest), only one from each row (its partner right beside it won't give you a second item, but the next row does, however close), and none while both hands are full. Boxes respawn after 0.6 s.
Odds depend on your position: leaders get small tools (Turbo, Rubber Duck, Segfault, Memory Leak, Infinite Loop), the middle gets Ctrl+Z, Fork Bomb, Thread and the first leader-hunting items, and the back of the pack gets catch-up (Overclock, malloc, Bubble, Pull Request, Ghost, Kernel Panic, and Fast-Forward for last place). The middle and back are deliberately given tools aimed at the leader rather than the car just ahead, so 2nd and 3rd don't spend the race fighting each other. Firewall is rare. Your position for the odds is your place and how far behind the leader you are, with the distance counting for more the further back you fall, so dropping well back brings more catch-up items, and Fast-Forward and Overclock more often still. The leader sometimes gets a Ghost to dodge what's coming.

There's no catch-up speed: karts behind catch up with better items, never by being faster. With **3 or 4 players** the chasers tend to fight each other while the leader drives off, so the odds lean on the leader: you count as far back sooner (2240 units instead of 3200), items you'd mostly use on the kart just ahead (Ctrl+Z, Fork Bomb, the ducks) come half as often Pull Request, aimed at the leader, a quarter more often, and the Bubble less often (0.7 of its usual share), and the further 1st is clear of 2nd, the fewer Firewalls and Ghosts it gets (down to half). In simulated equal-skill races the leader's average finishing margin over 2nd is about 2.5 s, and 1.75 s with these 3-4 player odds.
Fire with your **item key**.

### Who gets what
Your place decides which items you can get at all: 1st, the front half, the back half, or last (in a two-kart race 2nd is last). How far you are behind the leader decides the chances within that set. Items that catch up a lot when you use them well get more likely the further back you fall: Overclock aimed across a hairpin, Fast-Forward on a straight, Pull Request, Bubble, Kernel Panic. Items that only pay off when you're close enough to pass (Mega Duck, Rubber Duck, Ctrl+Z, malloc) fade out with distance. A close last place gets Mega Ducks; a far last place doesn't. An item with under 5% of the chances at your place and distance doesn't come at all (the rest scale up), so every roll is one of a handful of items that make sense there. Items that only work on a kart right by you (Rubber Duck, Segfault, Memory Leak, Infinite Loop, Fork Bomb, Firewall, Ctrl+Z) fade out as you fall back and stop coming at all once you're 1600 units (about 4 s) behind. The distance is measured on the road, the same on every track: "far" is 3200 units or more behind the leader (about 8 s at full speed), "halfway" 1600, and the chances slide evenly in between. In a two-kart race 2nd blends from the front-half pool when close to the last-place pool when far back, so a close duel still has Firewalls, Rubber Ducks and Segfaults; far back it stops a little short of the full last-place odds (80%), so the biggest catch-up items don't come quite so often. ## Tracks
- **Crab Harbor**: a crab's kingdom on a sunny beach by a little fishing harbour, big (about 40 s a lap): dunes with marram grass and sand fences, rock shelves with tide pools, umbrellas and sandcastles along the surf. The wide Harbour Quay along the moorings (the harbour town and its fish market, wooden piers with fishing boats tied up, and a lighthouse on the mole), the fast Lighthouse Sweeper, the narrow Groyne Chicane, the Whelk (a fast right that winds tighter, like a shell) and then the **Crab's Reach**, a long gentle curve along the surf where **the Borrow Checker** lives: a giant orange crab that scuttles sideways along the beach keeping pace with whoever's coming. Every few seconds it rears a claw over the road: the claw goes up, its shadow darkens on the road where it will land, between two red bars that flash faster as it comes, and 1.3 s later it slams down right across the road. Under it then, or driving into it while it's down, and it swats you toward the land and takes most of your speed (no worse than that: a Firewall saves you). It aims where it thinks you'll be (a human player more often than a bot), sometimes a little short, sometimes long: floor it if you can get past before it lands, or lift and let it land and lift off ahead of you. From your second lap it pinches with both claws, one aimed ahead of you and the other a little behind it, the back one landing a moment after the front one: lift when it rears and go as the first claw lands (lift too little and you meet the front claw, too much and the back one gets you). On the last lap it scowls. Then the Lobster Pot hairpin (a wide braking zone), the narrow Boardwalk flat out into the Wreck Hairpin (the hardest corner), the Kelp Esses, and the Shell Carousel: a 270° left that climbs over its own way in on a timber bridge, onto the line. Bots read the claws too: they beat it, wait for it, or lift and go through a pincer, and they don't fire boosts under one. Single item boxes out wide on the Lobster Pot's exit and on the Shell Carousel's outside wall: carry speed to take them.
- **Pharaoh's Jackpot**: a pharaoh's casino in the desert, about 37 s a lap (it was Snake Pit, and grew to twice the size). The road is edged in gold, with gold-and-lapis kerbs, past a pyramid casino with neon running up its edges, a sphinx, an oasis, obelisks ringed with neon hieroglyphs, card tables, coin piles and slot-machine sarcophagi. The item boxes and the gambling are kept apart. All the plain boxes are on the wide Strip (the start straight, the fastest on the lap), the bazaar: four rows of three, close together between striped market stalls, with a sandstone pylon either side at each end. That's where you go for items (fire one and grab another). The Strip ends at Pharaoh's Gate, a right that tightens into a hairpin (the hardest corner). Then a chicane, the Carousel (a long, fast 180° sweep round the bottom), Jackpot Avenue back up the middle into a double left, the Spike (a hairpin pointing up between two kinks) and the Staircase (four tight steps) back to the line. **Jackpots**: the rest of the lap is gambling only. At the apex of six corners (two in the chicane, before the Spike, after it, at the bottom of the Staircase and on its third step) three golden boxes sit on a gilded inside kerb, with an Eye of Horus painted on the road. Lift early and your line tucks in onto them; stay on the gas and you pass in the middle. Drive through one and a slot machine spins over your kart, its three reels stopping one after another: two alike pays an item and a little rush of coins (a short boost), three alike is the jackpot (an item in every free hand and a long coin rush), and a cobra is a bust: nothing, and your kart coughs to a crawl for a moment. About a fifth bust, a third hit the jackpot. No other box counts while the reels spin. On average the gamble about pays for the lift (the lift costs from a twentieth of a second to half a second depending on the corner); bots go for it now and then, more when they're behind or their hands are empty.
- **Duke's Volcano**: a road up and round a live **volcano**, about 35 s a lap. The cone stands in the middle of a mountain of old lava terraces, and the road winds round it. The **Ash Flats** start straight (the fastest on the lap, over the lava river on a steel bridge) ends at the **Obsidian Hairpin** (the hardest corner). Then the lower switchback back across the flank, through the river's lower ford flat out, into **the Funnel**: a flat-out right that tightens into a hairpin, so lift late and hard. Straight out of it you cross the upper ford at low speed, then the Flank Road to **the Kiln** (a medium left), the fast **Ash Sweeper** up the east side, the **Crater Lip** (a quick left-right-right-left dip in right under the summit), the long fast Rim bend, the **Basalt S** down the west side and the last corner back onto the flats. Every 8-12 s the volcano rumbles (the crater glows brighter and shakes) and erupts, hurling two to four big globs of lava onto the road, most of them aimed just ahead of a kart (a human player's more often than a bot's); they reach everything but the flats and the west side. Each one is its own lumpy splat, stretched the way it was thrown, with tendrils, and big enough to cover most of the road. From the moment one is thrown you can see it: a molten glob tumbling over from the crater, and its exact shape marked on the road where it will land, flashing and filling in over the two seconds it takes to come down. Be under it when it lands and your kart burns and melts down into the lava (about 2 s), then comes back up a little way back, at a standstill: a big penalty (a Firewall saves you). And it stays on the road as a pool that's all molten for the first second: drive into that and you melt down just the same. Then it crusts over from the edge in, the molten middle shrinking away, and the crust only slows you, less as it cools. So lift and let it land and crust over ahead of you (once there's room, the steering takes you round the molten middle), or floor it and get past before it lands. **The lava river**: a channel runs from the crater down the cone and the flank, across the road at the two fords, under the start straight and into a lava lake. Between eruptions only a thread of lava trickles down it and you drive straight over the crust. But about half the time an eruption spills lava over the crater's lip, and a surge runs down the channel: you see its white-hot front coming down the mountain, it reaches the upper ford a few seconds later and the lower one a second and a half after that, and floods each for just over a second (the striped posts at the ford flash red from when it's on its way until it has passed). On a ford while it's molten and you melt down, as in a pool. So when the lamps flash: floor it if you'll be across before the front gets there, or lift and let it go by (it drains quickly and leaves a crust you can drive on). At the upper ford you're coming out of the Funnel's hairpin, so you can't beat it unless you're already there, but waiting costs little; at the lower ford you're flat out, so beating it is easy if you're early and waiting costs a lot. Bots read the marks and the river too: they beat a surge, wait for it, queue behind a kart waiting at a ford, and never fire a boost toward one. Single item boxes on the outside of the bend at the top of the Ash Sweeper, under the summit, and out wide on the Basalt S: carry speed to take them.
- **Moonshot**: the grand final, the biggest map by far: **one lap** (always, whatever the laps setting; in a time trial too) from the Earth to the Moon and back, about 2 min for the winner. The bottom of the map is the top of the planet, its curved edge glowing blue with the air; above it black space full of stars; and at the top of the map the bottom of the Moon, its own curved edge facing the Earth across the gap. **On Earth** a spaceport on a sunny tropical cape: a deep blue ocean going turquoise over the sand, white surf, a beach with people under umbrellas, sandstone sea cliffs along the cape, a calm green lagoon with mangroves and salt marshes, and the big island beyond, with hammocks of dark round trees, pines, palmetto scrub and palms round mown lawns and buildings. From the line on Ocean Boulevard (now and then a breaker rolls in off the ocean across it: lift and let it break ahead of you, or floor it and get through first) the road climbs onto the **Cliff Road** along the cape's sea cliffs, fast and flowing (a left up onto the cliffs, a right, a left round a cove, then the fast cliff top), into the **Lighthouse Hairpin** at the cape's point: the hardest corner, at the end of the fastest stretch. Back across the cape to the **Causeway Leap** (the bascule bridge over the lagoon's channel is up: a ramp throws you across the open water, and a gauge shows the window: green lands you on the road with room to make the hard left beyond it (the top of the green is quickest), red too slow and you splash into the lagoon and start again from well back, orange too fast and you land wide (a little: into the dust at the bend; a lot: past the road's edge, a crash landing in the scrub that kills most of your speed and spins you), so you lift for it), then **the launch complex**: the launch tower's two **gantry arms** swing out across the road one after the other, hold and swing back (lift to arrive as they swing back, or get there before they come out; under one and you're knocked aside; its lamp is red while it's out), a rocket engine on its **test stand** fires across the road now and then (its igniters spark first, then the flame blasts across to the blast wall on the far side: in it you're slowed hard and shoved toward the far side, so get through before it lights or wait at its edge for it to die away), the **Step** (a quick right-left), and the **crawler-transporter** with a rocket on its back shuttling across the road between its garage and a pad on the lagoon's shore (lamps flash amber before it moves: beat it across or let it pass; into it and you're shoved off it and nearly stopped). A long left onto the **Crawlerway** over the lagoon, and at the top, on the dunes, the **Launch Row**: a rocket for every racer in the race (four karts, four rockets), in one line down the side of the road, each on its own pad with its flame trench and its red service tower, and a boarding ramp from the road's edge up to its hatch. **No braking**: drive in flat out. The first free ramp catches you as you reach it and throws you up it and into the rocket, at whatever speed you came in. They're taken in order along the line: the first kart there gets the first rocket, the next runs on to the next, so the front of the race is aboard first, lands nearest the Moon's gate and gains a little on the way. The ramp that will catch you lights up in your colour and the line takes you over to it. Moments later your rocket (a chunky cartoon rocket, its nose, fins and bands in your colour) lights up and goes, up through the clouds on a long flame, its booster falling away, a bright streak across space, and down on its legs onto its own pad on the Moon's landing field; you roll out through the field's gate in the order you got there. **On the Moon** (hard sunlight from the top left: every crater half in deep shadow, dark smooth maria, bright highlands, boulders) everything grips less. **Kepler Bowl**, a loop banked like a velodrome round the inside of its crater, and out over its own way in on a bridge: the outer half of the road climbs the crater's wall, so the faster you go the higher your line rides, gripping harder the higher it is (a blue band where the floor meets the wall, a red line and a blue one up it). Arrive hot and ride high round the wall, or lift and drop to the tight floor line: the fast racers (Duke, Docky, Octo) are quickest riding the wall flat out, the slow grippy ones (Bugdroid, Gopher) dropping to the floor. Then down into **Hadley Rille**, a narrow canyon cut into the plateau, its rock walls rising on both sides (one bright in the sun, one in deep shadow): a fast right into its mouth, the climb, a left onto the rim, an S, and a hairpin that tightens. There's no run-off down there: run wide and you hit the wall and grind along it, so with the Moon's low grip every lift point counts. Out of the hairpin onto **the mass driver**, a magnetic launch rail: a line of coil rings over the road. While it charges they light amber one by one from the far end back to the first ring (its five lamps count down); then it fires, all green, for a moment; then it discharges, red. Drive through the first ring on the green and you're flung down the rail at nearly twice your top speed; on the red it grabs you and kills most of your speed; while it charges nothing happens. A gauge over your kart shows the next few seconds of its beat and a marker at when you'd get to the first ring flat out: lift and the marker slides later. So floor it if you'll make the green, lift a little to arrive just as it turns green (too much and you're into the red), or just drive through while it charges. And launched, lift hard and early for **the catch**, the hard right at the end of the rail. Out of it west along the Moon's edge, the Earth hanging below, past a **rover** shuttling across the road on its ruts between two garage domes (lamps flash before it goes: beat it or let it pass), into **the Apennine Crests**: three round low-gravity crests, each with a turn just past it (a gentle crest into a fast right sweeper, a sharp one into a left, a third into a right that tightens). The Moon's gravity is weak, so the faster you take a crest the lighter you go and the less you can turn, and past its float speed you float right off it, flying straight on (no steering, no lifting) until you come down, in the turn and pointing the wrong way. Amber chevrons up the road's edges mark where a crest starts to go light, and a gauge over your kart shows the fastest you can take it and still be down in time: **green** you're down before the turn, **orange** you'll float into it. So lift before each crest, not on it (flat out costs about 0.5 to 1.5 s and a trip through the dust; lifting far too early about 1.5 to 2 s). The gentle one floats only the fastest racers; the sharp one floats everyone. Then the climb to the top of the Apennines, a hairpin over the top, and down to **the base**, a right to the **airlock**, two doors that slide shut from both edges of the road and open again one after the other (lamps green open, amber moving, red shut: time them; caught by one and you're stopped dead behind it), into the **hab**, a long white module whose roof hides the twisty run through it, and out to the **Return Row**: a capsule for every racer on its little gold ascent stage, in one line, taken the same way (in flat out, the first free ramp catches you, the first there gets the first capsule). Your capsule lifts off, falls across space toward the Earth, burns in through the air like a comet, opens a drogue and then three chutes in your colour, and splashes down in its own zone off the beach (a ring of orange floats; the first capsule home gets the zone nearest the slipway), and a little tug tows it in: you drive out of the hatch and up the slipway in the order you got there for **the drive home round the harbour**: a fast left out along the pier, a right onto the **outer mole** (a road on a breakwater out at sea; breakers wash over it from the ocean side: lift and let one break ahead of you, or floor it and get through first), the **Mole Head hairpin** round the harbour light, back along the **inner mole** into its long left that tightens as it comes ashore, and onto Ocean Boulevard to the line. Bots drive into the rows flat out, keep a boost for after the ride, lift for the Causeway Leap's window, time the arms, the test firing, the crawler, the rover and the airlock (racing a plan of their run past each, and waiting short of it when that's quicker), ride Kepler at a height that suits how good they are, take the canyon's corners at the Moon's grip, time the mass driver (racing a plan of their run in, the green worth lifting a little for, never the red), lift for each of the Apennine Crests, and wait out or beat the breakers.
- **Sakura Stack**: a figure-eight through a Japanese garden, about 29 s a lap: cherry trees in bloom with their petals drifting across the road, maples, cloud-pruned pines, stone lanterns, koi ponds, a five-storey pagoda and a raked-gravel garden. You cross the red bridge in the middle, loop round, and later drive back underneath it. The **Hanami Straight** (the start, the fastest stretch on the lap) ends at the **Blossom Hairpin** (the hardest corner, with a wide braking zone). Then the **Torii Turn** (a right out under a vermilion torii), the **Maple S** (a dab each way), the **Shrine Sweeper** (a fast double-apex left you can take flat in a grippy kart, a dab in a fast one), and the long diagonal flat out over the red bridge into the **Koi Curve**: a gentle left that suddenly tightens into a hairpin, so don't commit early. Up the narrow **Lantern Chicane**, round the **Carousel** (a long 165° right round the top of the garden), and down to the **drawbridge** over the koi canal. Every so often (at no fixed beat: each race has its own) the temple bell beside it rings and the red lanterns on its posts flash, and a moment later its leaf starts to lift on its chains to let the garden's boat through; it stands up for a moment and comes back down, about 4 s in all. While the leaf is still low it's a ramp: hit it fast enough and you leap the canal (let go of the gas at the lip for a trick and a boost on landing). The steeper it gets, the faster you have to be going, and soon it's a wall: arrive then and you bounce off it, spinning, and wait at its foot until it's down. On the way down it's a ramp again for a moment. So when the bell rings: if you're close, floor it and leap it; if you're further back, lift and roll up so you meet it as it comes down (you can leap it again then, still at speed). It's a long leaf over a wide canal, so the leap is a proper flight. Waiting at its foot costs up to 3.5 s; misjudging it and bouncing off costs another half second to a second on top. Then under the red bridge, the fast Sakura Kink and back onto the straight. Bots read the leaf too: they leap it when they'll make it (a worse one now and then misjudges it), roll up to it as it comes down, and don't fire a boost into it. Single item boxes on the inside of the Shrine Sweeper's exit (lift a touch for it; flat out you sail past) and on the Carousel's outside (carry speed round it).
- **Pyth Plateau**: a city of painted step pyramids on a sunny highland (a pale temple faced with blue feathered serpents and Pyth coiled in mosaic on its plaza, the red-and-ochre Pyramid of the Sun with gold suns on its tiers and fires burning on top, a ball court), with kerbs in Pyth's blue and yellow, and a new road every lap. The three roads share only the start/finish straight, which ends in a T, so the corner at the end of it is different every lap. A dotted line in your colour shows where your road goes, your route is highlighted on the minimap, and a route sign pops up in the event feed as you cross the line. Each kart follows its own lap's route, so the leader can be on a different road from the pack. Lap 1, **the Sun Road** (about 25 s, round the outside of the north): left at the T through the Sun Gate, a long sweeper you can just about take flat (the fastest karts dab), up the east side over the Sun Ramp (let go right at the lip for a trick and a boost), the Moon Bend (a fast left that tightens: carry speed in, lift as it closes), the Step (a quick left-right jog in the top straight), the longest straight on the lap into the Moon Hairpin (the hardest stop), then a right, a fast left and a left back onto the straight. Lap 2, **the Serpent** (about 21 s, the south): right at the T, a hard right where you were flat a lap ago, the Tail (a long fast right round the corner), then the Serpent itself, esses along the bottom that get tighter one after another (flat, flat, a dab, a lift, a lift) into the Jaws, a right hairpin, and an S back up to the straight. Lap 3, **the Temple Road** (about 20 s, inside the Sun Road): the Temple Hairpin just past the line, the slowest corner of the race at the end of the fastest run, then the Carousel (a long right round the temple courtyard), the Courtyard (a tight left that opens into a fast one: floor it early), the long top straight into the Coil (a fast left that tightens hard), a kink and the last left onto the line. Each lap suits someone different: top speed on the Sun Road, grip on the Serpent and the Temple Road. Built for 3 laps; on 5 it cycles. The Temple Road hides a secret, and on lap 3 it's hard to miss: a sun sign pops up in the event feed as you start the lap, the Pyramid of the Sun on the outside of the Temple Hairpin wakes up: golden light climbs its stair and runs along its ledges, the suns on its bands glow, its braziers flare and the temple on top shines, and the sun altar at the foot of its stair glows. The altar is past the end of the run-off, so only a kart that's blazing can carry on out there: fire a Turbo on the run down to the hairpin (or a Ghost just before it) and keep your foot in, and you blow straight through the hairpin and onto the altar. The whole pyramid blazes, a pillar of fire erupts from its top right off the top of the screen, the sky turns a burning orange, and you burst out as a giant phoenix of the sun with huge flaming wings, a blazing sun rising behind it: you soar high over the temple and the Carousel in a firestorm, burning feathers raining down and flames bursting along the ground beneath you, and come down like a meteor on the straight past the Carousel: a mushroom of fire, shockwaves, sparks raining and a scorched crater. Everyone within a big radius of where it lands is burned out (they sink, charred, and come back up a little way back at a standstill), unless they're phased out by a Ghost, on a Fast-Forward or round a loop, a malloc giant, or a Firewall soaks it up. As the phoenix dives, a big red target ring on the road shows where it'll hit, closing in for about a second: floor it to get clear or lift and stay out. About 3.4 s in the air, and still roughly 1.5 to 2.5 s quicker than firing that Turbo down a straight; mistime it and you're just out on the grass. No Turbo? A drift's mini-turbo counts too, and it works on every racer: keep the gas down into the Temple Hairpin, press drift about when you'd normally lift for it, hold it round the hairpin and let go as the sparks turn blue (within a tenth or two of a second; press it a little earlier and you can let go a little later). The drift gets you round the hairpin flat out, and the mini-turbo throws you wide off its exit while you're still hot, so you carry on out onto the altar: about 1.5 (Bugdroid) to 2.2 s (Tux, Cpp) up on the normal line, 1.1 to 1.7 s more than the best drift that stays on the road. Let go too early or too late and you're out on the grass flat out, about 1 to 1.7 s down. The track's Show more has a clip of it (Watch how).
- **Merge Bay**: very big, and the road forks twice, round a seaside town on an island in the bay (a cute toy island: sandy coves and rocky points all the way round, two islets offshore, a marina and a duck lake, a farm with cows, sheep and a windmill, a little town square with a fountain, a lighthouse out on a rock). At each fork the approach is painted as two lanes, the sharp road's in gold on the inside and the sweep's in white, with blue signs naming where each goes (CHICANE or POCKET, and SWEEP). Just past the apex, at the tip of the hatched gore, you're put on whichever road you're nearer: tucked into the gold lane and that's the sharp road, out in the white one (or off in the run-off) and it's the fast sweep around the outside. Once you're tucked in, the kart turns into the sharp road in one smooth arc, and your dotted line swings onto the road you're set for. Lifting early and just enough is the skill: too little and you're on the sweep, too much and you've thrown the time away. The sharp roads are the hard part: a five-bend chicane at the first fork (shorter, and faster only if you drive it clean) and a snaking pocket with a boost pad at the second (about even with the sweep; grippy karts gain, Duke loses). Each fork is judged on its own, so a lap can use either, both or neither. Bots pick a plan at each fork and stay out of the apex when they want the sweep. Waves roll in off the bay across two of its straights now and then: a foam line builds along the edge, then the breaker shoves anyone it catches toward land. A single item box on the inside of the first fork's chicane: stay tucked in for it.
- **Kernel Pretzel**: a pretzel of a lap round a live volcano, with a **long split**. A start straight with a ramp, then the road forks for nearly half the lap into two very different roads that take about as long. The last stretch before the fork is two lanes, painted all the way in: white arrows (and a loop) for the **Big Loop**, orange arrows (and a volcano) for the **Caldera**. Your line moves into a lane by your speed: flat out keeps you in the white lane; lift until you're slow enough for the Caldera's first bend and you drift into the orange one (once in, you stay in unless you speed up again). Where the lane divider runs into the striped gore, you're committed to the road whose lane you're in; your dotted line and the minimap show which. The **Big Loop**: a fast right, the Cinder Hairpin at the end of a straight, then the **Kicker Park**, a wide straight up the east side with a line of kickers down its right-hand lanes and a clear lane by the left kerb, ending in a hairpin. Hold the gas and your line rides the kickers: each one throws you into the air (no steering, no speeding up or slowing down up there), and let go of the gas right at a lip for a trick and a boost when you land, so a rhythm of taps chains them, faster and faster. Lift for real (longer than a tap) and your line moves over and you drive round the next one. Fly the last kicker fast and you come down hot at the hairpin with no room to brake: lift before it (and fly it slowly, or go round it) to make the corner. Then the top straight, a sweeper, and back down over the Loop's own entry on a bridge. The **Caldera** winds round the **volcano** (it erupts every 10-14 s and its lava reaches only this road; read the marks where its globs will land, as on Duke's Volcano). Halfway round, the **Ash Run** cuts straight down the volcano's flank, but it's choked by the **Tinder Grove**: a barricade of dead trunks and thornbush. Now and then an eruption lobs a fireball into it instead of the road (a ring of fire marks the spot), the thicket goes up in flames, and for about six seconds there's a charred gap, embers glowing on the road and dimming as its time runs out while the burnt trunks either side lean in, until they topple across and it's shut again. Chevrons in its mouth show it: gold while it's open, flashing while it burns, a red cross while it's shut. While it's shut your line keeps you out; from the moment the fireball's in the air you can lift and tuck into it like a fork's sharp road, but arrive while it's still burning, or as the trunks come down, and you plough into it and bounce back onto the long way all but stopped (a Firewall smashes through). The Ash Run saves about a second; ploughing in costs about three. The long way is a set of esses out to the west. The roads meet again on the bottom straight before the Pigtail (a loop that tightens over a narrow bridge), then a long run up the west side into a hairpin onto the start straight. A single item box out wide on the exit of the corner past the Pigtail's bridge: carry speed out of it to take it.
- **Pipeline Pass**: not a loop (penguins skate on the frozen pond, polar bears roam the snow). A summit-to-plaza descent in three stages, with a gate between each (the lap counter counts stages). The wide Summit straight. Two hairpin Switchbacks with maintenance bots crossing. The Viaduct, a fast straight you later pass under. The Ledge: a narrow sweep on the cliff. The Carousel at the top right. The Pigtail, a loop over a narrow bridge. The long Dam straight, a chicane, then the Crevasse: a ramp you must hit flat out. The Plaza at the bottom is wide so stopped karts stay out of the way. About a minute. Single item boxes round the outside of the second Switchback hairpin and out wide on the left into the Pigtail (carry speed), and on the inside of the fast left just after you pass under the Viaduct (lift a touch).
- **Heap Space**: a space station laid out as a figure-eight, about 35 s a lap. The wide launch rail, a long diagonal under the orbital overpass into a hard hairpin, the Asteroid Belt esses, and the Event Horizon (a long left that suddenly tightens). Then back over the overpass into the **Low-G dome**: a moon ramp over a crater flies you more than twice as far as a normal ramp, with a bend just past the landing. Too slow at the lip and you drop into the crater and restart well back; a little too fast and you land in the dust past the bend; much too fast and you fly within reach of the moon out there: its gravity pulls you into orbit, you spiral down onto it and pop, and respawn at a standstill on the road past the bend. The two ringed gas giants beside corners pull too: run wide off the road along either of those corners and you're caught the same way, and respawn where you ran off. Lift to the right speed before the lip (you can still trick it, but the landing boost comes right before the bend). Last, the **Docking Arm**: a narrow finger with a hairpin at the end, and a **wormhole** across its mouth. While the wormhole glows, driving in pops you out of the exit across the arm at the same speed, skipping the hairpin at its tip (about 2 s). It opens for 2.5 s out of every 7 (the ring round it counts down: amber until it opens, cyan while it's open), so floor it to make the window, lift to arrive as it opens, or don't bother and take the arm. Single item boxes on the outside of the hairpin under the overpass and out wide past the Docking Arm: carry speed to take them.
- **Assembly Line**: a factory floor laid out as a serpentine, about 34 s a lap. The wide Conveyor Hall straight, a fast sweeper and two kinks up the left, then the loading bay into the **Stamping Line**: three hydraulic presses that slam across the whole road on a beat (lamps flash just before each drops). Get caught under one and you're flattened: stopped dead and squashed out of it. They're timed as a green wave: at the right speed you pass each one in its gap, flat out you beat the first and meet the next coming down. So floor it and gamble, or lift and ride the wave. Next, the **Crucible**: a long, very wide 180° round a pot of molten metal, with a ladle hanging over it from a crane. Get caught in it and you're scorched: spun out of it with most of your speed gone. A single item box out wide on the double-apex right's first apex: carry speed into it.
- **Cron Canyon**: a red-rock gorge with flat-topped mesas, a dry wash running down into a chasm, and the Cron railway cutting straight across it corner to corner (over the wash on a timber trestle, out through a rock cutting), about 38 s a lap. - **Cryo Caldera**: subterranean frozen crater (penguins by the hot springs, polar bears out on the snow) with full low-grip ice physics (0.80× grip) where feathering the throttle and drifting lines is essential. Features robotic snow-sweeper bots patrolling the ice, a ski jump launching over a deep glacial crevasse, an elevated ice viaduct bridge, steaming geothermal crater ponds and geysers that erupt every so often, and road widths ranging from a massive frozen lake arena (1.45× road width) down to a razor-thin ice shelf ledge (0.70× road width). - **Async Atoll**: a coral atoll in hurricane season, about 32 s a lap. The wide Beach Road start straight, the Palm Sweeper and a kink up the east island, then the **Seawall Carousel**: a banked 270° right round the harbour that climbs over its own entry on a bridge. The banking turns the usual rule round: the outside line is the fast one. Carry speed and the kart rides up the wall and holds the corner (the red line is where the banking starts, the blue one the top lane); lift and it drops down to the flat apex and has to go slower; too hot even for the top and you go over the seawall into the sea, and you're fished out at a standstill. Then the **Causeway**, the longest straight, out across open water between two low breakwaters, with a kink in it. And every so often a **hurricane** tracks right across the map: it comes in off the open sea on one side, crosses on a gently curving path and goes out the other side, and a while after it's gone the next one comes, from wherever it likes (each race has its own). The part along the road carries you: a tailwind makes you quicker over the ground (floor it and ride it), a headwind holds you back. The part across the road shoves you sideways, harder the faster you go: past a certain speed the steering can't hold the line and you're pressed to the edge, knocking and scraping along it in a shower of sparks and losing speed until you can steer off it (it can't blow you off the road: on the Causeway the breakwaters hold you, anywhere else the kerb). The open Causeway gets the full force of it, the islands a little less, and on the banked seawall the wall shelters you from the part across. So as the eyewall comes over your bit of road: in a crosswind lift a touch to hold the line, or carry the speed and pay for it on the wall (about even); ride a tailwind flat out; and the eye is dead calm. Bots ride tailwinds, lift a little for the strong crosswind they'll meet (and never boost into it or into the eyewall) and ride the banking at a height that suits how good they are. A single item box out wide on the Breakwater Hairpin's exit: carry speed out of it.
- **Ruby Ruins**: a jungle temple in ruins, about 35 s a lap. - **Recursion Ridge**: an alpine pass with **real hills**, about 37 s a lap. Climbing slows you: you pick up speed more slowly and top out lower. Grinding up a climb on the gas you kick up gravel. Going downhill you speed up, well past your kart's usual top speed on a long descent (the wind streams past your kart and rushes in from the edges of the screen), and when you lift you slow down less. The **Valley Straight** (the start, over a stone bridge across the gorge, a lake and the cable car's valley station beside it) into the **Meadow Sweeper**, a long flat-out left, then the Foothills to the Foothill Bend and **the Switchbacks**: four hairpins stacked up the mountain, all different. Along the snowy **Ridge** over the summit (an observatory, the cable car's top station, a waterfall below) to a **long split**, painted as two lanes. The wide white-water river carries you all the way down through the middle of the map, bobbing, rolling, ducking under in the rapids and knocking off boulders, for about 14 seconds you can't do anything about (the camera rides with you), then slows into a calm pool beside the Valley Straight: the last of the current drifts you into the shallows, you bump aground, roll up its sandy beach dripping (past a little jetty with a rowboat), shake the water off and drive back onto the road, just short of the line: 6.5-7 s worse than a clean flight. Too fast and you sail past the marked landing slope, crash on the road and skid straight on off the outside of the corner, bouncing across the Ramp's last stretch into the **spill**, a white-water stream down the west side of the Schuss: a splash, an eddy, a short fast ride, and it washes you up on a little beach beside the Schuss further down: about 3.6 s worse than a clean flight (it used to be a plain crash, about 1.3 s). In between you land on the long landing slope (green lines, a red line at its end), and where on it depends on your speed: the slow end of the window comes down at the top, the fast end near the bottom. You see the kart arc through the air off the kicker: hang-glider wings in its own paint pop out of its sides as it leaves (nose up), vapour trailing off the tips, rising and growing as it soars over the face with its shadow sliding away down the rocks below, rocking a little, then they fold away as it comes nose down onto the slope with a burst of dust (coming down short or flying long, the wings just flap uselessly and crumple). Coming up to the lip a gauge over your kart shows the window (red short, green good, orange long) with a needle at your speed: lift until it sits in the green (the fast karts have to; the slow ones just hold it flat), and in the air a ring shows where you'll come down. A clean Cliff beats the Ramp by 0.7-1.8 s (1.75 s for Gopher, 0.7 s for Docky: the Ramp is all top speed); the spill costs about 3.6 s and the torrent far more, and the run-up is a bad place to get hit. Both meet at the top of **the Schuss**, the steepest, fastest descent on the lap, which ends in **the Kehre**, a left hairpin at its foot (braking downhill: lift early), then back up beside it into **the Avalanche** (a right that tightens) onto the Valley Straight. Bots plan their braking with the hills, take the Cliff more often the better they are (about half to three quarters of the time), aim a little over the middle of the window, since short costs so much more than long (a weaker bot less precisely: it misses now and then), and never fire a boost on the way to the lip. A single item box on the outside of the second Switchback hairpin, the left that tightens: carry speed through it.
- **Race Condition**: a steampunk clocktower with real heights, about 35 s a lap. You start on the cobbled **Plaza** at the foot of the tower, take the **Escapement** (a left hairpin) in through the tower's gate and climb inside it, on wooden floors between turning brass gears: the **Lower Gallery** climbing west, the **Ratchet** (a right that tightens into a hairpin), the **Upper Gallery** climbing east, and the **Mainspring**, a 360° left coil round the tower's great blue-steel spring that climbs over its own way in on a bridge. Up the **East Face** (a boost pad) onto the top of the tower and the **Dial**: a fast 120° balcony sweeper round the great clock face. The tower's clock has gone haywire and runs backwards, fast: its **minute hand** (a brass spade at one end, the counterweight at the other) comes round the way you're going, a little slower than you, about every 3.5 s. Each end comes down over the arch at the top of the East Face and sweeps right across the balcony to the far arch, dragging sparks (past the arches it lifts clear of the road). - **Neon Overflow**: a rainy cyber-city at night, about 35 s a lap alone (38 in the wet). City blocks under neon: dark rooftops with air conditioners, skylights, gardens, helipads and billboards, their lit windows and blade signs facing down the street, neon totems along the pavements, holograms turning over the plazas, steam out of the vents, a monorail gliding over the rooftops on its beam, and the Spire, a tower lit up like a crown, inside the Cloverleaf; the car park full of parked cars under the Corkscrew. **Rain comes and goes**: every race has its own weather (some start in the rain), with a roll of thunder, a flash and a rain sign as each spell comes in. As it rains the road darkens and turns to a mirror (the signs' colours smear across it, the karts throw up spray) and **every corner grips less** (up to a fifth less when it's soaked), so you lift earlier; after it stops the road dries off over the next half a minute or so. But the karts wipe the water off where they drive: a **dry line**, a darker matte groove, forms along the racing line through the race and grips much better, while off it the road stays wet, so going off the line for a box or a pass costs grip (in steady rain the line is worth about a second a lap by the third lap; a lot more rain washes it back). Where the water pools in the dips there are **puddles**, deeper the wetter it is: hit one too fast and you **aquaplane**: for half a second your tyres float, nothing steers, the tail steps out and you slide on wide, usually off the road. Lift under the speed and you cross it safely (just over is a twitch, flat out a proper slide). Drifting in the wet slides further, charges your mini-turbo faster and spins you out sooner. The lap is a figure of eight on three levels: **Neon Boulevard** (the start, wide) to **Plaza Corner**, a U-turn hairpin at the end of the fastest stretch, **the Ramp** (a right) and up **the Cloverleaf**, a loop of three quarters of a turn that **tightens as it climbs** (arrive hot and it catches you at the top) and comes out over its own on-ramp onto **the freeway**, high on pillars over the city: **the Merge** (a kink where a slip road joins, flat out), **the Overpass Sweeper** (a long fast left: a dab of a lift, or carry the speed and run wide onto the box on its exit), then down over the Boulevard to **the Storm Drain**, a fast right in the dip at the foot of the off-ramp where the first puddle lies (you arrive faster than your top speed), along the diagonal to **the Gutter** (a right onto Garage Street) and into **the Corkscrew**: a multi-storey car park the road climbs as a coil, **two turns round over itself**, each turn a fast sweep up the open side and a hairpin loop at the back, the second tighter than the first, its levels painted on the ramps. Out at the top onto the car park's roof at **the Roof Corner**, along **the Rooftop** and over **the Gap**, a ramp across the alley to the next building's roof: hit it at around four fifths of your top speed or more and you fly it, any slower (or bubbled, or spun on the way) and you **drop into the alley** and start again from well back (let go of the gas right at the lip for a trick). Then down **the Fire Escape** and through **the Sump**, a fast S in the dip under the freeway (the second puddle: the hill hands you more speed than the S wants) and onto the Boulevard. Bots plan their corners for the grip they expect on their line (a weaker bot expects less of the wet than there is, arrives hot and has to lift), lift for the puddles the better they are (from about half the time to nearly always), and don't fire a boost into one.
- **Gateway 8080**: a busy, sunny airport, about 35 s a lap. Jets with their airlines' tails taxi out and take off all round the road, one every 9-14 s (each race has its own timetable), past a glass terminal with jets parked at its gates, a control tower, a hangar and aprons full of tugs pulling luggage carts. The **Runway Straight** (the start, the longest) runs beside the runway, and a jet lining up there runs its engines up and rolls the other way down it: its blast (you see the heat shimmer and the streaks rushing back) blows straight down the straight. Be just behind it on the gas as it goes (the end of its run-up and the first seconds of its roll) and it **slingshots** you way past your top speed: wind streams past your kart, a tailwind sign warns you one's coming, and it's worth 0.6-0.8 s down the straight if you're there at the right moment. - **Gradient Descent**: a ski run from the summit to the lodge in the valley: not a loop but one long run down a snowy mountain (the lap counter counts stages), about 1 min 40 s for the winner. Pine forests either side, piste poles along the edges, a chairlift up the side of the mountain. On snow you **can't really slow down**: lifting bleeds speed off at under half the usual rate, gravity keeps pulling, and only the air holds you back, so lift early. Out of the start hut down the Summit Schuss, the Corduroy (three carving turns, each tighter than the last), then the **Pine Glade**: the piste opens into a snowfield six times a road's width round one long, sweeping bend, clumps of pines all over it with lanes between them, just as thick on the outside of the bend as on the inside, and trees right along both edges: there's no way round them and no side that's a free pass, every line through the glade has to dodge. The Ledge is a shelf barely wider than your kart across a cliff face: a wall of rock and boulders up to the piste on one side, a sheer drop on the other, the valley far below with wisps of cloud under you. Shorter than the piste round the outside, but lift a touch where it rounds the spurs and don't hit the wall fast; run off the edge and you fall, and climb back onto the piste further up from a standstill. Or stay on the wide piste round the outside, which has its own **kicker line**: three kickers right across it, let go of the gas right at each lip for a trick and a pop of speed, land one without and you scrub. Hit all three and it's as quick as a careful run along the Ledge; miss them and it's slower. Then **the Slalom**: a long straight of sixteen red and blue poles to weave round, drifting left, right, left (the drift the way you're swinging carves; an arrow on the snow ahead of your kart and the drift button's mark beside it show which way a drift would carve right now, bright when that's the way you're swinging), in a rhythm that changes: a steady run, a tight pair, a long, wide delay gate, an offset pair, a wide one and a steady run to the end (clip a pole and it knocks you, go inside one and you've missed the gate), the Elbow (a fast left that tightens), **the Moguls** (a long field of snow bumps round a long right, laid out evenly in staggered rows like a real mogul field: no lane runs straight down it, so you weave between them with the drift, round a bump in one row and between two in the next. Hit a bump fast and it launches you: up in the air, shoved hard off its side, tumbling, a big share of your speed gone; let go of the gas just before one and your legs soften it, but it still costs you. Hold the drift to carve in, lift to hold your line, let go to drift out, and thread the gaps (never drifting runs you into the trees along the edge, drifting all the way into the ones along the other). With drift off, lifting carves you in instead. It narrows back to the piste in a long funnel, then the Last Schuss through the finish arch to the lodge.

**Front-runner rating.** Some maps suit whoever is in front more than others. Each track's **How it plays** on the map (and its Show more in an online room) has a 0-10 **Front-runner** meter (10: the leader is very hard to catch) with the reason in a line; the map's **Front-runner ratings** switch shows every track's at once, and can sort the tracks by it. It's measured, not guessed: 96 bot races with items per track (how often the kart leading at a third, half and two thirds of the race wins, and how often the lead changes), plus what a leader can do on purpose that bots don't: drop a mine where it hurts most (before a jump, the lava, a train) and fire a Turbo where it gains most (a secret shortcut). Moonshot (8.1), Duke's Volcano, Pipeline Pass and Ruby Ruins (6.5-6.6) favour the leader most; Pharaoh's Jackpot (1.4), Race Condition, Recursion Ridge and Kernel Pretzel (2.1-2.4) least, mostly because their boxes keep the leader under fire. Scores within about 1.5 of each other are too close to call.

The big tracks use a camera that follows the human players and zooms out to keep everyone in shot, on one shared screen. A minimap in the corner shows the whole lap. Online, every screen frames every player the same way, yours first.

The HUD has a card per player (mascot, place, lap, speed, the item you hold), the standings top right, and an **event feed** at the bottom middle of the screen: no words, just a row of icon chips, the newest sliding in from the right while older ones move left and fade after a few seconds (four at most). A hit reads left to right: who fired, the item, whom it hit (e.g. Gopher, duck, Pyth). Your laps show as a green flag with the lap number and the last lap as the white flag; a finish as a gold cup for the winner, a medal with the place for a player. Hazards ahead get a yellow sign (train, wormhole with a green or red light for open or shut, logic gate likewise, a hurricane coming in, its wind on the road ahead with which way it'll push you, eruption, low gravity, presses, slag, banking, drawbridge, the rocket's launch pad, fork, a new route) and whatever caught you a red one. Chips about your own kart have a rim in your colour. Bots' fights among themselves stay out of it.

**Results.** When every player is over the line the results come up over the race, which carries on behind them: the karts still out fill in their times as they finish. The winner stands big on a turntable in their colour ("You win!" when it's you), with each of your players' places and the gap to the winner, and any new personal best on the track (record time or best lap) called out in gold. It fits a phone either way up and an iPad; the rows come in with a quick stagger, and not at all with Reduced effects or your system's reduced motion.

## Cup mode
Pick **Cup** on the title, then on the map pick a ready-made cup or **Your own cup** and click 3 to 5 tracks in the order you want to race them: they fill your cup's numbered slots (and the track cards get numbered badges); click a track again, or its slot's ✕, to take it out. Your custom cup and the cup you chose are remembered (choosing a ready-made one keeps yours; clicking a track goes back to it). Or choose one of the ready-made cup cards (a trophy and its tracks in order), then set the laps and bots and press **START CUP**: **Coast** (Merge Bay, Async Atoll, Sakura Stack, Crab Harbor), **Sun** (Cron Canyon, Ruby Ruins, Pharaoh's Jackpot, Pyth Plateau), **Magma** (Assembly Line, Kernel Pretzel, Duke's Volcano), **Peak** (Pipeline Pass, Cryo Caldera, Recursion Ridge, Heap Space) and **City** (Gateway 8080, Race Condition, Neon Overflow, from the airport by day to the streets at night in the rain): every track but the giant finale in exactly one, each cup building to its showpiece; plus the **Kernel** cup, the grand final: three of the best back to back, then the giant one-lap finale and the ski run down to the lodge (Crab Harbor, Pyth Plateau, Duke's Volcano, Moonshot, Gradient Descent).

The races run back to back with the same racers, laps and bot skill. Every kart scores by its finishing place, humans and bots alike: 10/7/5/3 with 4 karts (10/6/3 with 3, 10/5 with 2). Ties go to more wins, then the better last race. After the first race the grid starts in reverse order of the standings, so the leader starts at the back. The loading screen shows the cup so far (done tracks ticked, this one lit) and who leads. The results screen shows the winner, the race with the points each place scored (+10, +7...) and the cup standings, which play out quickly: each kart's points pop out of its result and fly into its total, then the rows slide into the new order with how many places each moved (▲2, ▼1) and each one's places so far. The cup's progress is a row of dots at the top (done, this one, still to come) and the footer shows the next track's picture and how many races are left. `Enter`, `Space` or a click skips straight to the final table, and then `Enter` goes on: **Next race** instead of a rematch, and after the last race a podium (the top three standing in 3D on the steps, the champion in the middle hopping for joy, and confetti) with the final table and the cup's tracks; **Race this cup again** (`R`) or **Menu**. `Esc` (or Quit cup, top left) goes back to the menu and abandons the cup. With Reduced effects or your system's reduced motion, the standings show their final state straight away.

Cups work online too: the host switches the room's Track panel to **Cup**, anyone in the room picks a ready-made cup or builds one (each track tagged with who put it in), and the host presses **START CUP**. Everyone sees the same standings after each race (players by their names, bots by their racer), the host presses **Next race** (or `Enter`) for everyone, and after the last race everyone gets the podium. A player who leaves mid-cup keeps their points, marked "left", and a bot drives their kart from then on; someone who joins mid-cup races once it's over. **End cup** (or `Esc` mid-race) takes everyone back to the room.

## Time trial
Pick **Time trial** on the title, your racer, then a track, and press **START TRIAL**. It's you (P1's racer) alone: no bots, no item boxes, no bits and **no obstacles**: nothing comes at you or shuts the road, so every lap is down to you. No trains on Cron Canyon (the crossing gates stay up), the Garbage Collector bots and the Moon's rovers parked on the verge, no lava from Duke's Volcano or Kernel Pretzel's (its globs and the lava river's surges), no boulders on Ruby Ruins, the crab asleep on its beach, no waves, no hurricanes, the jets sitting at their gates (no taxiing, no blast), the presses parked up, the slag never poured, the drawbridge down, the clock stopped (its minute hand up over the arches, the pendulum hooked back off the road), the logic gate held open, Kernel Pretzel's thicket a charred gap that stays open, and on Moonshot the gantry arms back, the test stand quiet, the crawler and the rover waiting at the end of their tracks and the airlocks open. The road and everything that's part of it stays: jumps and ramps, the cliffs and their windows, banks, forks and splits, walls, belts, pads (and the clocktower's strike doubling them), the ski run, the Rollers, the wormholes and their cycle, the mass driver, the rocket rows, the secret shortcuts, and the rain on Neon Overflow (the same every attempt). **Only your fastest lap counts**: on a circuit it's hot laps, as many as you like from the grid (`Esc` or the pause button ends it), every lap timed, and your one time on the track is your best single lap, never a total. Where a lap on its own means nothing the whole run is the time instead, shown the same way: on Pyth Plateau, whose road changes every lap, a run is its 3 roads, and on Pipeline Pass and Gradient Descent it's the whole way down. Moonshot is always one lap: that one grand lap is the time. (Records were always the best single lap on a circuit, so old ones carry straight over.)

Your best is big and gold at the top of the screen (**BEST LAP**), with a live **VS BEST** beside it: how far ahead of (green, −) or behind (red, +) your best lap you are right now, worked out from where your ghost was at this point. The HUD card has the clock, the lap and your last lap; the panel on the right lists this session's laps (the fastest in gold). Your best lap is saved per track with its **ghost**: a see-through, pale blue copy of your kart and mascot that drives that lap again on every later one, starting as you cross the line, so you can see exactly where you gain or lose. It shows on the minimap too. Every quarter lap a split pops up under the target, and again at the line for the whole lap. Beat your best and the clock turns gold with **NEW BEST** on the card, a gold burst and a chime, and that lap becomes the ghost from the next one.

`R` restarts straight from the grid, `Esc` ends the trial (so does **End trial** on the pause card). The results show your racer on its turntable with your fastest lap this time, big, against your best (a new best says how much it beat the old one by) and against a friend's ghost you raced; that lap's **sectors** (its four quarters; a run's laps or stages) against your old best's and the friend's, with the ideal lap from your best sectors of the session; then every lap (the fastest gold, a new best marked). **RETRY** (`Enter` or `R`) retries at once, the next track's button (`N`) goes on, **Share ghost** (`S`) copies your ghost's link, **Menu** (top left, `Esc`) goes back. Track cards in Time trial mode carry your best time on their picture, and the map's left side shows it for the picked track. Records and ghosts set with drift on are kept apart from the ones set with it off (the trial's box, its loading screen and results say which), so you never race a ghost from the other game. Times set before time trials lost their obstacles are kept apart too (nothing is deleted): they're not raced against, and until you set a new time on a track its box shows your old one ("with obstacles it was 0:36.42"). Records and ghosts stay in this browser (a ghost is ~3-12 KB; if storage fills up, the oldest other tracks' ghosts go first, never their times). Time trial is offline only: the online room has no such mode (but it shows everyone's bests, below). The time trial remembers its own track, apart from the single race's.

**Friends' ghosts.** **Share my ghost** on the time trial box (or **Share ghost** on the results, `S`) copies a link with your best ghost on that track packed into it: the track, your racer, your time, drift on or off and the whole lap, about 1,000-1,300 characters for a 30-40 s lap (about 25-35 characters a second of lap; Moonshot and Epoch Expressway's grand laps about 3,000). The first time it asks for your name (the same one the online room uses). The link opens the web version (or, on a LAN server, that server's address): whoever opens it lands on the menu in Time trial on that track with a card, "Racing Pia's ghost: 0:31.42", and **RACE IT**. The friend's ghost drives in pink with their name under it, beside your own pale blue one, and the target at the top shows your best, theirs (in pink) and the live gap **VS PIA**; the splits are against them too. Beat it and the results offer **Send yours back to Pia**. A link set with drift on switches your Drift setting on to match (and off for one set without). Friends' ghosts are kept in this browser per track (six at most, one per friend: a new one from the same friend replaces theirs) and listed as **Rivals** on the time trial box: click one to race it (click it again to race only your own ghost), ✕ to forget it. A link from a track layout that's since been redone, a link from before time trials lost their obstacles (its ghost waited for trains and lava: ask for a new one), or a cut-off link, says so instead.

**The leaderboard.** Everyone's best time on each track, kept on the game's own server (whoever runs `npm run play` or `npm run server`: the same one online rooms use), so friends see each other's times from anywhere that reaches it, not only in the same room. It's on the time trial box and on the results: rank, name, racer, time and the gap to the fastest, the top 20 per track (one time per name; your own row marked), with drift and without kept apart like your records. **Race** on someone's row fetches their ghost and makes it your rival on that track, as a ghost link does (on the results it goes straight into a retry against it). A new best goes on by itself, under your name (the one the online room and ghost links use; with none yet, the results ask once); **Post my best** puts one there that isn't yet. The server keeps the boards in `.kk-board.json` next to it (`npm run play` in the main checkout; `KK_BOARD_FILE=<path>` puts it elsewhere) and checks what comes in: a sane time, a short name, and a ghost that opens and really is that time on that track. The web version has no server of its own, so it shows no leaderboard, unless it's opened with `?server=<address>` (remembered; `?server=` with nothing forgets it), which points it at a game server that can be reached (it has to be https for an https page to ask it).

**In an online room**, the room's track (a single race on a picked track) shows **Time trial bests**: everyone's best time trial lap there with the room's drift setting, fastest first, with who set it and on what racer. They're swapped by themselves when someone joins and when the track or drift changes (each player's best and its ghost go once, never during a race). **Save ghost** keeps a friend's ghost as one of your rivals on that track, as a link would, to race in Time trial later.

## Fight mode

All ten racers fight (**Tux**, **Duke**, **Gopher**, **Ferris**, **Pyth**, **Octo**, **Docky**, **Swifty**, **Cpp** and **Bugdroid**), each rebuilt around three big moves and an evade of their own. The setup screen is a fighting game's select screen: each side's fighter big on its own stage under a spotlight, in its side's colour, with its name plate, showing off its moves, and the roster in the middle with each side's cursor on it (**P1** red, **P2** blue), a big VS over it and the maps under it. Move your cursor with your own keys (two players on one keyboard: P1 `WASD`, P2 the arrow keys; one player: `WASD` or the arrows), or click a fighter: a click picks for the side whose card you last clicked (P1 at first), a right-click for the other. A fighter you pick spins in with a hop and a cheer. Each side's card has three tabs: **Fighter** (what it's like, its stats: Health, Weight, Speed, Power, Reach, and its passive), **Moves** (the three moves and the evade: the key that player presses, the move's icon and name, and whether holding the key aims or steers it; click one for a big card with a live clip of it, what it does, its **tell** (how you see it coming) and its **answer** (how to beat it), `←` `→` for the others) and **Keys** (that player's keys drawn as on the keyboard, with each move's name). Your picks are remembered.

**Rounds and wins.** Before each round the two fighters' portraits slam in from the sides on bands in their colours, VS flashes where they meet with the round number over it, both cheer and get ready in their own pose (Tux crouched for the torpedo, Duke with a hand up to snap), then 3, 2, 1 drum in and an impact star starts the round. A knock-out freezes the picture for a moment (a flash, lines closing in on the one going down) and the camera eases in on them; the round's winner hops and confetti pops off them. **Maps.** Pick one on the setup screen (a card per map with a little top-down picture): **Sky Ring** (the floating stone ring: open ground, a low wall all round), **Pillar Court** (a square yard with four stone pillars: they block walking, knockback bounces off them like the wall, and you can hide behind one), **Data Bus** (a long narrow hall: no circling round, two crates, and the side lanes run like conveyor belts, one each way) and **Cold Boot** (an ice rink round a hot core: ice in the middle where you slide, and two heat vents that glow, then flare every 7 s and knock anyone on them down with a bit of damage).

**Winning.** Best of 3 rounds, about 60 s each. Health only: win a round by knocking the other one's health to zero (a KO); nobody can be knocked out of the arena. Its edge is a low solid wall: knocked into it (or into a pillar or crate) hard enough, a fighter slams off it, takes a little extra damage and goes down. When the clock runs out it's sudden death: every hit does more damage, ×1.5 rising to ×3 over 20 s (the clock shows it), until someone goes down.

**Three moves and an evade.** No jabs, no shield, no stamina: each fighter has three big moves of their own, one key each, and an evade on the fourth key (`Space` for one player). Big hits and hard wall slams **knock them down**: they fall flat, can't be hit lying there, then hop up (untouchable for a moment, see-through). **Controls.** Fight needs a keyboard or a gamepad (no touch controls: on a phone or tablet the Fight tile is greyed out). One player: `WASD` or the arrow keys move, `J` `K` `L` the three moves, `Space` (or `;`) the evade. Two players on one keyboard, with `Y U H J N M` left free between their hands: P1 `WASD` move, `F` `G` `T` the moves, `V` the evade; P2 the arrow keys, `L` `K` `I` the moves, `.` the evade (or the numpad: `4` `5` `6` the moves, `0` the evade). Gamepads (the first pad is the first player): stick or d-pad, A X B the moves, LB or RB the evade, Start pauses. During a fight each player's card shows their health and their moves' icons with cooldowns; their keys (or pad) sit in the bottom corner with each move's icon on its key and its name over it, lighting up as they're pressed and sweeping round while it comes back. `Esc` or `P` pauses (Resume, Restart, Quit). The results show who won, the rounds and a few numbers per fighter (damage, hits, counters, knockdowns, wall slams), with **Rematch** (`Enter`) and **Back** (`Esc`).

**Online.** In an online room the host can switch the mode to **Fight**. The room then shows the two fighters as the setup screen's big cards side by side, P1 red and P2 blue, VS between: who's on each, their fighter turning on its stage, its stats, passive and moves (click one to watch it). The first two players with a keyboard or a gamepad fight (one alone fights a bot, whose level and fighter the host picks on its card); you pick your own fighter on your card, and the other card changes as they pick theirs. Everyone else watches, listed under the cards (a phone or tablet always watches). The host sets **Supers** and presses **START FIGHT**; everyone else sees a "Waiting for the host" chip. After the fight the results say who beat whom on what; the host has **Rematch** and **Back to room**.

## Music
Every screen has its own chiptune, generated from a seed: the menu, the results, and one song per track. The race music speeds up on your final lap. `Backspace` mutes everything.

## Players
Up to 4 humans on one keyboard, three keys each (gas, item, drift), spread out depending on how many play:

| Players | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Q` `W` `E` (or `Space` `Enter` `Shift`) | | | |
| 2 | `Q` `W` `E` | `O` `P` `[` | | |
| 3 | `Q` `W` `E` | `V` `B` `N` | `[` `]` `\` | |
| 4 | `Q` `W` `E` | `C` `V` `B` | `N` `M` `,` | `O` `P` `[` |

**Gamepads** work too: the first four pads plugged in are P1 to P4 (online, the first pad is you). In a race `A` is gas, `B` or the left shoulder or trigger drift, `X` or the right shoulder or trigger the item, `Start` pauses; in the menus, on the results and on the pause card the d-pad or left stick moves, `A` presses, `B` goes back and `Start` goes on.

Players are told apart by their tag as well as their colour (the P1-P4 tags over the karts, on the cards and in the standings; with 2+ players the minimap's dots carry their numbers, and on a touch screen each item button its player's tag). Each player's keys show in their colour on the racers screen (under their racer, "In the race") and on the loading screen, and online in your own card in the room.

On a touch screen each player gets a zone with an item button, and a pause button sits in the top right corner. AI fills the grid to 4. On a phone the HUD (the cards, standings, minimap, feed and countdown) is drawn smaller, scaled from the screen's short side, so it doesn't cover the road; the touch buttons stay a thumb's width.
`P` pauses (`Tab` with 2+ players, since `P` is a player key; the race also pauses when the tab is hidden), `Backspace` mutes, `Esc` quits to the menu. The pause card has **Resume**, **Restart race** (`R`: the same grid on the same track from the start; in a time trial **Start again**) and **Quit to menu** (in a time trial, **End trial**) to tap, click or pick with the arrow keys, and the settings you'd want mid-race: sound, screen shake, reduced effects and drift (a drift change counts from the next race, or a restart), plus **Full screen**.
`F` (or the **Full screen** button in the menu's top bar, the room's and the pause card) switches full screen where the browser can (not on an iPhone, whose Safari can't; Add to Home Screen does it there). On phones and tablets the screen stays on while you race or wait in a room (where the browser has the Wake Lock API), the race never zooms, scrolls or pops up a menu when you tap or hold, and the HUD and the touch buttons keep clear of the notch and the home bar.

### Online (same Wi-Fi)
Up to 4 players, each on their own laptop or phone, on the same network:

1. One person starts the game's server: `npm run dev` (or `npm run server`, which builds the game and serves it on port 8080 without the dev tools; `PORT=3000 npm run server` picks another port). Both listen on the local network and print a "Network" address such as `http://192.168.1.23:5173/`.
2. They pick **Online** on the menu's title, type a name and press **Host a room** (the room opens with the track, laps and bots of their last one). The room screen shows a four-letter room code (no I, L, O or Q, so nothing to misread) and the room's link to send, e.g. `http://192.168.1.23:5173/#join=KRTW` (click it or **Copy** to copy it).
3. Everyone else opens that link on the same Wi-Fi (or scans the room's **QR code**: the button beside **Copy** shows the link as one, big, for a phone's camera) and is straight into the room, nothing to press: with their saved name, or the first time a made-up one (Pixel Pete, Async Ace...) that the ✎ beside their name in the room changes (it's remembered after that, and the host may change theirs the same way). It works on a phone, on a page that already has the game open, and mid-race (you wait in the room for the next one). Or they pick **Online** on the menu, type a name and the code and press **Join**. The web copy of the game (GitHub Pages) can't host or join rooms: a room link opened there says so and asks for the host's link (or paste it there and press **Go**). The room is one screen: on the left the code and the address to share, everyone in the room on their own turntable in their colour, the bots and the chat; on the right your racer, the track or cup and the race settings; **START** at the bottom, and **Leave room** (the host: **Close room**) at the top left. Each picks a racer and anyone can pick the track, or **Random** for a different track every race (or, in a cup, choose a ready-made cup, add and take out tracks); the track's card carries the name of whoever picked it. Two or more of you may pick the same racer (each racer card carries the tag of everyone on it). Everyone sees the whole room: the code and the address, who's racing on what, every track with the one picked, the cup and the race settings. The host alone sets single race or cup, laps, drift (on or off for everyone; it starts as the host's own Drift setting), bots and bot skill (shown greyed for everyone else), and presses **START RACE**. To keep it friendly, everyone but the host can change their racer once every 2 seconds and the track (or cup) once every 2 seconds (the host changes things as often as it likes). Your pick shows at once; just after a change that section dims with a ring counting down, and a card you click then shakes and shows how long is left.

Rooms are for one local network: everyone has to be on the same Wi-Fi (or wired network) as the host's machine. `KK_SHARE_IP=<address> npm run play` makes the link use a particular address, if the machine has more than one.

Everyone plays with `Space` (or `Q`) for gas and `Enter` (or `W`) for items, or the touch zone and item button on a phone. Your screen follows your own kart. The host's machine runs the race; the others send only their keys and draw what the host sends (20 times a second, smoothed in between, and carried on for a moment when a patchy Wi-Fi holds one up; each key press is sent again with the next few, so a quick tap isn't lost), so the host should keep the game in view: a hidden tab stops the race for everyone. The host and each player also open a direct link between their two browsers (WebRTC, on your own network: nothing goes out to the internet) for the race, where one lost Wi-Fi packet doesn't hold up the rest; if it can't be made (a firewall, say), the race just goes over the game's server as before. There's no pause online. **Link quality:** in the room each player's card has a chip with three bars and their round trip to the host (green under 90 ms, amber under 220, red above), so you can tell whose Wi-Fi is struggling; in a race, if your own connection is poor (the host's updates arriving late or unevenly), a small red Wi-Fi sign shows at the bottom of your screen, so a jerky race gets put down to the connection. If a player leaves, a bot drives their kart home; if the host leaves, the room closes for everyone. If a player's connection drops (a Wi-Fi blip, an iPad's screen going off), their screen says **Reconnecting…** and tries the same room again with the same name for 20 seconds, then puts them back in the room on their racer; a race that's on goes on without them (a bot drives their kart home) and they're in the next one (in a cup, from the next cup). **Leave room** on that card gives up at once. After a race the host takes everyone back to the room for the next one; `Esc` does the same for the host mid-race, and leaves the room for anyone else. The host can also run a cup (see Cup mode): the races follow one another with the standings in between, and a podium at the end. After a single race the host's results have **REMATCH** (Enter: the same track again), **Next** (N: the next track, with its picture, or another random one on Random) and **Back to room**, so you can go again without the room in between; everyone else's say they're waiting for the host, and the chat sits beside the results. **Close room** (the host) or **Leave room** is top left.

**Chat.** Everyone in a room can chat. The room has a **Chat** panel under who's racing (and the results and the podium have it too): type and press `Enter` (or **Send**), or tap one of the quick reactions (**gg**, **nice!**, **oops**, **rematch?**). Names are in each player's colour. Whoever joins sees the last lines said before they came in (the room keeps 50). In a race, new lines show for a few seconds in a small stack on the left under the player cards, then fade; press `T` (or `/`) to type one: while the line is open your keys go to the chat only (your gas lets go as it opens, so keep your thumb ready), `Enter` sends it and `Esc` closes it. On a phone or tablet, tap the chat button above the item button. A soft blip says someone else wrote. A line is at most 120 characters and you can send one about every 0.7 seconds; it's plain text (no links or formatting). If you've scrolled away from the chat in the room, a new line peeks in at the bottom of the screen.

The first time, macOS or Windows may ask whether Node may accept incoming connections: allow it, or friends can't connect. Playing online needs one of those two servers: a copy of `dist/` on a plain web host plays offline only.

## Racers
Two or more players may pick the same racer, offline or online (the bots still go for racers nobody picked). Then each player's kart is painted in their own colour (P1 red, P2 blue, P3 green, P4 orange) under their P1..P4 tag; their HUD card, their row in the standings, their dot on the minimap, their head in the event feed and their dot in the results and cup standings are in that colour too, and a cup scores each of them on their own. A bot on a racer a player has is a darker, plainer version of it with a grey dot. A racer of your own looks as it always does.

Every racer drives their own kind of car. Bigger cars are harder to shove around but are bigger targets and can't hug the inside of a corner as tightly; small ones are nimble but get bumped about. Stats are balanced by simulation: alone, everyone's lap pace is within about ±2%, and in random four-kart races everyone's average finishing place is within about 0.2 of each other (except Cpp, whose stats the user set: all speed, no grip).
