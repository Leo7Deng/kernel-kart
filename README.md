# Kernel Kart

One-button mascot kart racing in the spirit of *Formula Cartoon All-Stars*. Plain JS modules on a canvas, built with Vite.

**Play it on the web:** https://leo7deng.github.io/kernel-kart/ (single races, cups, time trials; online rooms still need someone running the game's server, below). `npm run publish:web` updates it.

**Run it:** `npm install`, then `npm run play` (or `npm run dev`) and open the address it prints. `play` serves a clean copy of the last commit and follows each new one, so a reload never catches a half-made change. `npm run build` puts a hostable copy in `dist/`; `npm test` replays every track headless and checks nothing changed. **What's new** (title screen, `C` on the menu, or the results screen) is a changelog built from git history, so it needs no upkeep: one version per day of commits, newest first, numbered `<major>.<day>.<commits that day>` (1.0 began on 2026-10-18 with the batch of new maps and items; days count up from there). It lists only game changes (no docs or tests), tagged by kind (Maps, Balance, Items, Racers, Racing, Online, Menu & HUD, Look & sound, Fixes) and filtered by chips along the top; a red badge counts changes since you last opened it, marked "new" in the list. Beside it, **Settings**: **Style** for the menus, HUD, item icons and boxes and item effects (tracks, cars and mascots look the same in all; it switches at once): **Classic**, glossy and glowing; **Classic Flat**, the same layout, colours and fonts minus gradients, glows, shine and rainbows (solid fills in Classic's indigo, red-orange and gold, simple soft shadows, at most a gentle two-tone, inked and extruded titles kept); **Racing**, like a race game's menus: black and carbon panels, racing red and signal yellow, heavy italic condensed capitals, skewed number plates (also for the places), red and yellow stripes, chevrons, the odd chequered strip; **Premium**, warm and polished: espresso panels with a faint warm sheen, amber and copper accents, warm off-white type in Montserrat and Manrope, soft shadows, generous rounded corners. Only Classic has glows or rainbows. **Text size** (Normal, Large, Extra large) enlarges the menus, results, online room, pause card and HUD (on a phone only as far as it fits), for a small phone or a TV across the room. Then music and effects volume, mute (in step with `Backspace`), screen shake, flashes, reduced effects (fewer particles, same race), Show FPS, **Drift** (on by default: the third key, see How it plays; off removes the key, its hints and the touch button: the classic two-button game), and three off by default: **3D models (experimental)** (each racer a cartoon 3D model turning with its kart, so you see its side, back or face by heading), **Map cover art (experimental)** (the map screen's track cards and cup thumbnails show a picture of each map, the crab looming over the beach, the volcano erupting, the train at the crossing, instead of its layout; the big preview keeps the layout) and **3D cars (experimental)** (separate, to judge cars and riders apart: each car a cartoon 3D car in its own paint, number and stripes (Gopher's red kart, Octo's white kart in its Git livery, Ferris's monster truck, Pyth's striped sports coupé, Tux's bobsled, Duke's formula car, Docky's lorry and container, Swifty's dune buggy, Cpp's hypercar, Bugdroid's electric kart), seen from a little to the south so you see tops and the near side, front wheels (the bobsled's front runners) steering, brake lights on, exactly their size on the road; a racer two players share is in each one's colour, as in 2D). Saved in the browser; `Esc` closes the panel.

**The menu** is a few screens in a row. A short loading screen (logo and a kart riding a bar; a second or two the first time, about half a second after, as track pictures are kept in the browser; any key or tap skips it) fades into **the title**: the logo and four big tiles, **Race** (one race, 1 to 4 players and bots), **Cup**, **Time trial** and **Online**. How to play, Settings, In the works and What's new sit in the corner. Race, Cup and Time trial go to **Who's racing**: players, bots and bot skill on a bar at the top, then a big slot per player in their colour (P1 red, P2 blue, P3 green, P4 amber) with their racer on a slow turntable (the 3D model riding its kart), its stats, and READY once picked; every racer is below, tagged with the players who have it (players may share). Each player picks with their own keys, shown on their slot: gas for the next racer, drift for the one before, item for ready (all ready, item again goes on); or tap a slot, then a racer. **The map** is next: the picked track big on the left with what it's about, **How it plays** (its mechanics in full, the **Front-runner** meter, a Watch how clip where there is one), laps and Drift; every track on the right (**Random** first: a new track each START, while Rematch keeps its pick), chips for one ready-made cup's tracks or **Recent** (your last 8, offline or online, newest first), a **search box** narrowing to names containing what you type (`/` jumps to it; `Enter` or `↓` to the first match, `Esc` empties it) and a **Front-runner ratings** switch putting every track's rating on its card (sortable highest or lowest first). In a cup the right side has the ready-made cups and **Your own cup**; in a time trial the left has the track's records, rivals and Share my ghost. **START** is bottom right, your racers bottom left (click to change them). **Online** has its own screen: your name, Host a room or Join with a code. **Back** (top left) or `Esc` goes back a screen; the steps at the top (Racers, Track) jump between the two. After a race the menu opens on the map, so another race is one `Enter`. All by keyboard: `Tab` between a screen's parts, arrows across racers and tracks, `Enter` or `Space` picks, `Enter` on the current pick goes on (Next, START); `H` How to play (driving and controls, the items, the item odds chart, as tabs), `S` Settings, `C` What's new. Everything is thumb-sized (44 px or more), text follows the browser's text size, and each screen fits a phone: on its side the racers sit beside their slots and the map's text folds under How it plays. The browser remembers mode, players and racers, track, your own cup or the ready-made one, the time trial's track (kept apart from the race track), laps, bots and skill, the Front-runner switch and its order. Online it remembers your name and racer (picked for you when you host or join, even if someone has it too), and as host your last room (track, laps, bots, skill, single race or cup), which your next room opens with.

## How it plays
- **Hold gas** to accelerate; the car steers itself around the track.
- **Rocket start.** The gas counts during the countdown. Start holding as the last light flickers green (the last third of a second before GO, or on GO) and you launch on a short boost with exhaust flame. Earlier and you burn out: wheelspin in smoke, slow away. Just after GO is a plain start. Every boost (pads, tricks, the rocket start, Turbo, a drift's mini-turbo) all but jumps you to its speed from cruising speed. While held, your kart revs on the grid (shakes, puffs exhaust), so you see it's held. The head start is the same for a quick launcher as a slow one. Bots try it too, better ones succeeding more often.
- The car follows one smooth line, no lane switching. It always wants the inside of a corner, but only as far in as the arc it can make at your speed.
- **Lift early and the line tightens** onto the apex: a shorter way round. Letting go also gives extra grip at once, so lifting mid-corner pulls you in.
- **Arrive hot and it stays out in the middle.** Too fast even for that and the car drives its widest arc, off the road. No sliding, no spin: you simply miss the corner.
- The run-off (grass/sand) drops everyone to the same crawl and the car steers back to the nearest edge of the tarmac. It's only so wide, so you can't cut across to another part of the track.
- Item boxes and nearby cars nudge the line a little: toward a box when your hands are free, to one side of a slower car ahead.
- Cars are solid: rear-end someone and you both feel it; side contact shoves the lighter car. Everyone takes slightly different lines and moves over to pass, so the pack spreads out.
- **Drafting** behind another car gives you extra speed, so the pack stays close.
- **Drift** (on by default, off in **Settings** for the classic two-button game; a third key: `E`, or `Shift` alone; `[` for P2 with two players, `N` and `\` for P2 and P3 with three, `B`, `,` and `[` for P2-P4 with four; a DRIFT button on touch). Hold it and the kart drifts toward the inside of the next corner: the rear steps out, it grips half as hard again, and its line tucks in further than grip alone allows, so you carry speed through a hairpin or tuck onto an inside box or bit. On a straight it moves your line toward the next corner's side instead (aim for a box or bit there); no steering wheel: only ever that way. It costs speed: a little in a corner, more on a straight (down to 90% of top speed). Held too long near top speed it strains (the kart shudders) and spins you out. Through a corner the rear-wheel sparks go blue (0.4 s), orange (0.85 s), then purple (1.4 s); let go for a mini-turbo, bigger each colour (+12/17/22% top speed for 0.5/0.8/1.1 s and a kick). Running off, a hit or a jump loses the charge. Bots drift too (with drift on): they pick slow corners long enough to charge a spark, brake for the extra grip, press just before the corner and let go as it straightens. Better bots drift more and time it well (Ruthless nearly every corner, hanging on for the next colour when it's a moment away; Normal about half); Chill ones drift a few, let go late or early, and now and then hold too long and spin. None drift near hazards, forks, ramps and crossings. A player who drifts every corner well still gains on a Normal bot. A mini-turbo counts as a boost for the secret shortcuts, so drifting wide onto one on a purple turbo takes it.

## Ramps, tricks and cannons
A **ramp** (yellow and black lip) throws you into the air, further the faster you go. In the air you can't steer or change speed: you land where you were pointing, so a ramp taken mid-corner lands you in the grass.
**Trick**: let go of the gas right at the lip (within a quarter of a second) and the kart spins in the air and boosts on landing. The lift costs a little speed on the way in: entry speed for exit speed.
A ramp before a **crevasse** is a **cannon**: a fixed flight to the far side, but only if you hit the lip above about 80% of your kart's top speed. Slower and you drop in, lose everything and restart from well before the ramp. Tricking a cannon is the gamble: the lift can leave you under the gate. Bots floor it for cannons and trick ramps about as often as they're good.

## Secret shortcuts
Some tracks hide a secret far out in the run-off on the outside of a corner, where the steering never takes you: look for something glowing. Running over it does nothing (it only stirs) unless you come in **blazing**: on a boost (Turbo, Overclock, a boost pad, or one that just ran out), or, depending on the secret, phased out by a Ghost or sliding on a Memory Leak's oil. Then it takes you (any part of your kart touching it is enough) and puts you back on the road further up the lap at a set speed. Some sit past the end of the run-off, reachable only blazing. Nothing can hit you on the way. It's a gamble, not a free skip: you must fire your boost into the corner instead of down a straight and keep your foot in while the run-off bleeds your speed; fire a little early or late and you end up on the grass. Hit it and it saves a second or two. Good bots go for it now and then.

## Bits
Little glowing chips lie on the road in short lines: at the apex of the slow corners (lift early to tighten onto them), at every other slow corner far out on the exit instead (stay on the gas to run wide onto them, risking the run-off: worth it?), out on the exit of a few fast sweepers (carry speed over them, or off the road if you overdo it) and down the middle of the longest straight. Each one held adds 0.4% to your top speed, up to **10** (a badge on your card counts them, gold when full; a full kart leaves them for others). Any hit (a spin-out, an item, a melt-down, a moon, a squish) throws **3** of yours out behind you for whoever comes past; you can't retake your own for a couple of seconds. A bit comes back 6 s after it's taken; thrown ones fade after 10 s. Bots lean toward a bit near their line, the better ones more.

## Items
Three or more rows of **?** boxes per lap (more on a long lap), mostly pairs; drive through one to grab an item. About a third of the rows are a single box, picked per track and set in a corner where your line depends on your throttle: one on the outside (wide on a corner's exit, or round a carousel's outer wall) is for a kart that carries its speed through, one on the inside of a fast corner for a kart that lifts a touch and tucks in. The start straight, rows where roads meet again and rows before a big hazard stay pairs. On a track with more than one road (a new route every lap, or a split), a row on a stretch only one road uses is a single box down the middle; shared stretches keep their pair. On a few tracks one row is a single box out wide on a fast corner's exit: carry speed to run wide through it, or lift and tuck in past it. You can hold **two**: the one you'll fire next (shown big the moment you grab it) and one in reserve (the small badge), which moves up when you fire. You take one box at a time (the closest), one per row (its partner beside it gives no second item, but the next row does, however close), and none with both hands full. Boxes respawn after 0.6 s.
Odds depend on your position: leaders get small tools (Turbo, Rubber Duck, Segfault, Memory Leak, Infinite Loop), the middle gets Ctrl+Z, Fork Bomb, Thread and the first leader-hunting items, and the back gets catch-up (Overclock, malloc, Bubble, Pull Request, Ghost, Kernel Panic, and Fast-Forward for last). The middle and back get tools aimed at the leader, not the car just ahead, on purpose, so 2nd and 3rd don't spend the race fighting each other. Firewall is rare. Your position for the odds is your place plus how far behind the leader you are, distance counting more the further back you fall, so dropping well back brings more catch-up items, and Fast-Forward and Overclock more often still. The leader sometimes gets a Ghost to dodge what's coming.

There's no catch-up speed: karts behind catch up with better items, never by being faster. With **3 or 4 players** the chasers tend to fight each other while the leader drives off, so the odds lean on the leader: you count as far back sooner (2240 units instead of 3200), items mostly used on the kart just ahead (Ctrl+Z, Fork Bomb, the ducks) come half as often, Pull Request (aimed at the leader) a quarter more often, the Bubble less (0.7 of its usual share), and the further 1st is clear of 2nd, the fewer Firewalls and Ghosts it gets (down to half). In simulated equal-skill races the leader's average margin over 2nd is about 2.5 s, and 1.75 s with these 3-4 player odds.
Fire with your **item key**.

### Who gets what
Your place decides which items you can get at all: 1st, the front half, the back half, or last (in a two-kart race 2nd is last). Your distance behind the leader decides the chances within that set. Items that catch up a lot when used well get likelier the further back you are: Overclock aimed across a hairpin, Fast-Forward on a straight, Pull Request, Bubble, Kernel Panic. Items that only pay off close enough to pass (Mega Duck, Rubber Duck, Ctrl+Z, malloc) fade with distance: a close last place gets Mega Ducks, a far one doesn't. An item under 5% of the chances at your place and distance doesn't come at all (the rest scale up), so every roll is one of a handful that make sense there. Items that only work on a kart right by you (Rubber Duck, Segfault, Memory Leak, Infinite Loop, Fork Bomb, Firewall, Ctrl+Z) fade as you fall back and stop at 1600 units (about 4 s) behind. Distance is measured on the road, the same on every track: "far" is 3200 units or more behind the leader (about 8 s at full speed), "halfway" 1600, sliding evenly in between. In a two-kart race 2nd blends from the front-half pool when close to the last-place pool when far, so a close duel still has Firewalls, Rubber Ducks and Segfaults; far back it stops at 80% of the last-place odds, so the biggest catch-up items come a bit less often. ## Tracks
- **Crab Harbor**: a crab's kingdom on a sunny beach by a little fishing harbour, big (about 40 s a lap): dunes with marram grass and sand fences, rock shelves with tide pools, umbrellas and sandcastles along the surf. The wide Harbour Quay along the moorings (the harbour town and fish market, wooden piers with fishing boats, a lighthouse on the mole), the fast Lighthouse Sweeper, the narrow Groyne Chicane, the Whelk (a fast right winding tighter, like a shell), then the **Crab's Reach**, a long gentle curve along the surf, home of **the Borrow Checker**: a giant orange crab scuttling sideways along the beach to keep pace with whoever's coming. Every few seconds it rears a claw over the road: its shadow darkens where it will land, between two red bars flashing faster as it comes, and 1.3 s later it slams down across the road. Under it then, or driving into it while it's down, and it swats you toward the land and takes most of your speed (no worse: a Firewall saves you). It aims where it thinks you'll be (a human more often than a bot), sometimes a little short, sometimes long: floor it past before it lands, or lift and let it land and lift off ahead. From your second lap it pinches with both claws, one aimed ahead of you and one a little behind it, the back one landing a moment after the front: lift when it rears and go as the first claw lands (too little and you meet the front claw, too much and the back one gets you). On the last lap it scowls. Then the Lobster Pot hairpin (a wide braking zone), the narrow Boardwalk flat out into the Wreck Hairpin (the hardest corner), the Kelp Esses, and the Shell Carousel: a 270° left climbing over its own way in on a timber bridge, onto the line. Bots read the claws too: they beat it, wait for it, or lift and go through a pincer, and don't fire boosts under one. Single item boxes out wide on the Lobster Pot's exit and on the Shell Carousel's outside wall: carry speed to take them.
- **Pharaoh's Jackpot**: a pharaoh's casino in the desert, about 37 s a lap (it was Snake Pit, and grew to twice the size). The road is edged in gold with gold-and-lapis kerbs, past a pyramid casino with neon up its edges, a sphinx, an oasis, obelisks ringed with neon hieroglyphs, card tables, coin piles and slot-machine sarcophagi. Item boxes and gambling are kept apart. All the plain boxes are on the wide Strip (the start straight, the lap's fastest), the bazaar: four rows of three, close together between striped market stalls, a sandstone pylon either side at each end. Go there for items (fire one and grab another). The Strip ends at Pharaoh's Gate, a right tightening into a hairpin (the hardest corner). Then a chicane, the Carousel (a long, fast 180° sweep round the bottom), Jackpot Avenue back up the middle into a double left, the Spike (a hairpin pointing up between two kinks) and the Staircase (four tight steps) back to the line. **Jackpots**: the rest of the lap is gambling only. At the apex of six corners (two in the chicane, before the Spike, after it, at the bottom of the Staircase and on its third step) three golden boxes sit on a gilded inside kerb, an Eye of Horus painted on the road. Lift early to tuck onto them; stay on the gas and you pass in the middle. Drive through one and a slot machine spins over your kart, its three reels stopping in turn: two alike pays an item and a little rush of coins (a short boost), three alike is the jackpot (an item in every free hand and a long coin rush), a cobra is a bust: nothing, and your kart coughs to a crawl for a moment. About a fifth bust, a third hit the jackpot. No other box counts while the reels spin. On average the gamble about pays for the lift (a twentieth to half a second depending on the corner); bots go for it now and then, more when behind or empty-handed.
- **Duke's Volcano**: a road up and round a live **volcano**, about 35 s a lap. The cone stands amid a mountain of old lava terraces, the road winding round it. The **Ash Flats** start straight (the lap's fastest, over the lava river on a steel bridge) ends at the **Obsidian Hairpin** (the hardest corner). Then the lower switchback back across the flank, through the river's lower ford flat out, into **the Funnel**: a flat-out right tightening into a hairpin, so lift late and hard. Straight out of it you cross the upper ford at low speed, then the Flank Road to **the Kiln** (a medium left), the fast **Ash Sweeper** up the east side, the **Crater Lip** (a quick left-right-right-left dip right under the summit), the long fast Rim bend, the **Basalt S** down the west side and the last corner onto the flats. Every 8-12 s the volcano rumbles (the crater glows brighter and shakes) and erupts, hurling two to four big globs of lava onto the road, most aimed just ahead of a kart (a human's more often than a bot's); they reach everything but the flats and the west side. Each is its own lumpy splat, stretched the way it was thrown, with tendrils, big enough to cover most of the road. You see it from the throw: a molten glob tumbling from the crater, and its exact shape marked on the road, flashing and filling in over the two seconds it takes to land. Under it when it lands and your kart melts down into the lava (about 2 s), then comes back up a little way back at a standstill: a big penalty (a Firewall saves you). It stays as a pool, all molten for the first second: drive into that and you melt down too. Then it crusts over from the edge in, the molten middle shrinking, and the crust only slows you, less as it cools. So lift and let it land and crust over ahead (once there's room, the steering takes you round the molten middle), or floor it past before it lands. **The lava river**: a channel runs from the crater down the cone and flank, across the road at the two fords, under the start straight and into a lava lake. Between eruptions only a thread of lava trickles down and you drive over the crust. But about half the eruptions spill over the crater's lip, and a surge runs down the channel: its white-hot front comes down the mountain, reaches the upper ford a few seconds later and the lower one a second and a half after, and floods each for just over a second (the ford's striped posts flash red from when it's on its way until it has passed). On a ford while it's molten, you melt down as in a pool. So when the lamps flash: floor it if you'll be across before the front arrives, or lift and let it go by (it drains quickly, leaving a drivable crust). At the upper ford you're leaving the Funnel's hairpin, so you can't beat it unless already there, but waiting costs little; at the lower ford you're flat out, so beating it is easy if early and waiting costs a lot. Bots read the marks and the river too: they beat a surge, wait for it, queue behind a kart waiting at a ford, and never fire a boost toward one. Single item boxes on the outside of the bend at the top of the Ash Sweeper, under the summit, and out wide on the Basalt S: carry speed to take them.

The bottom of the map is the top of the planet, its curved edge glowing blue with air; above, black space full of stars; at the top the bottom of the Moon, its curved edge facing the Earth. **On Earth** a spaceport on a sunny tropical cape: deep blue ocean going turquoise over the sand, white surf, a beach with people under umbrellas, sandstone sea cliffs, a calm green lagoon with mangroves and salt marshes, and the big island beyond, with hammocks of dark round trees, pines, palmetto scrub and palms round mown lawns and buildings. Back across the cape to the **Causeway Leap** (the bascule bridge over the lagoon's channel is up: a ramp throws you across the water, and a gauge shows the window: green lands you on the road with room for the hard left beyond (top of the green is quickest), red is too slow and you splash into the lagoon and restart well back, orange is too fast and you land wide (a little: into the dust at the bend; a lot: past the road's edge, a crash landing in the scrub that kills most of your speed and spins you), so you lift for it), then **the launch complex**: the launch tower's two **gantry arms** swing out across the road in turn, hold and swing back (arrive as they swing back, or before they come out; under one and you're knocked aside; its lamp is red while out), a rocket engine on its **test stand** fires across the road now and then (igniters spark first, then the flame blasts across to the blast wall opposite: in it you're slowed hard and shoved toward the far side, so get through before it lights or wait at its edge for it to die), the **Step** (a quick right-left), and the **crawler-transporter**, a rocket on its back, shuttling across the road between its garage and a pad on the lagoon shore (amber lamps flash before it moves: beat it across or let it pass; into it and you're shoved off and nearly stopped). The first free ramp catches you and throws you up into the rocket at whatever speed you came in. The ramp that will catch you lights in your colour and the line takes you to it. Moments later your rocket (chunky and cartoon, nose, fins and bands in your colour) lights and goes, up through the clouds on a long flame, booster falling away, a bright streak across space, and down on its legs onto its own pad on the Moon's landing field; you roll out through the field's gate in arrival order. **On the Moon** (hard sunlight from the top left: every crater half in deep shadow, dark smooth maria, bright highlands, boulders) everything grips less. **Kepler Bowl**, a loop banked like a velodrome round the inside of its crater, and out over its own way in on a bridge: the road's outer half climbs the crater wall, so the faster you go the higher your line rides, gripping harder the higher (a blue band where floor meets wall, a red line and a blue one up it). No run-off: run wide and you hit the wall and grind along it, so with the Moon's low grip every lift point counts. Out of the hairpin onto **the mass driver**, a magnetic launch rail: a line of coil rings over the road. While it charges they light amber one by one from the far end back to the first ring (its five lamps count down); then it fires, all green, for a moment; then discharges, red. Through the first ring on the green and you're flung down the rail at nearly twice your top speed; on the red it grabs you and kills most of your speed; while charging nothing happens. A gauge over your kart shows the next few seconds of its beat and a marker for when you'd reach the first ring flat out: lift and the marker slides later. So floor it if you'll make the green, lift a little to arrive as it turns green (too much and you're into the red), or just drive through while it charges. Launched, lift hard and early for **the catch**, the hard right at the rail's end. Then west along the Moon's edge, the Earth hanging below, past a **rover** shuttling across the road on its ruts between two garage domes (lamps flash before it goes: beat it or let it pass), into **the Apennine Crests**: three round low-gravity crests, each with a turn just past it (a gentle crest into a fast right sweeper, a sharp one into a left, a third into a tightening right). In the weak gravity, the faster you take a crest the lighter you go and the less you can turn, and past its float speed you float right off, flying straight on (no steering, no lifting) until you come down in the turn pointing the wrong way. Amber chevrons up the road's edges mark where a crest goes light, and a gauge over your kart shows the fastest you can take it and still land in time: **green** you're down before the turn, **orange** you'll float into it. So lift before each crest, not on it (flat out costs about 0.5 to 1.5 s and a trip through the dust; lifting far too early about 1.5 to 2 s). The gentle one floats only the fastest racers; the sharp one floats everyone. Then the climb to the top of the Apennines, a hairpin over the top, and down to **the base**, a right to the **airlock**, two doors that slide shut from both road edges and open again in turn (lamps green open, amber moving, red shut: time them; caught by one and you're stopped dead behind it), into the **hab**, a long white module whose roof hides the twisty run through it, and out to the **Return Row**: a capsule per racer on its little gold ascent stage, in one line, taken the same way (in flat out, the first free ramp catches you, first there gets the first capsule). Your capsule lifts off, falls across space toward the Earth, burns in like a comet, opens a drogue then three chutes in your colour, and splashes down in its own zone off the beach (a ring of orange floats; the first home gets the zone nearest the slipway), and a little tug tows it in: you drive out of the hatch and up the slipway in arrival order for **the drive home round the harbour**: a fast left out along the pier, a right onto the **outer mole** (a road on a breakwater out at sea; breakers wash over from the ocean side: lift and let one break ahead, or floor it through first), the **Mole Head hairpin** round the harbour light, back along the **inner mole** into its long left tightening as it comes ashore, and onto Ocean Boulevard to the line. Bots drive into the rows flat out, keep a boost for after the ride, lift for the Causeway Leap's window, time the arms, the test firing, the crawler, the rover and the airlock (racing a plan of their run past each, waiting short of it when that's quicker), ride Kepler at a height suiting their skill, take the canyon at the Moon's grip, time the mass driver (racing a plan of their run in, the green worth lifting a little for, never the red), lift for each Apennine Crest, and wait out or beat the breakers.

- **Recursion Ridge**: an alpine pass with **real hills**, about 37 s a lap. The road has a height everywhere, and you see it: terraces rise up the screen, each with its rock face below, ridges shadow the slopes, pines give way to rock and then snow, mist hangs on the slopes and the valley floor lies hazy blue far below. Up the Switchbacks each leg stands on a stone retaining wall over the one below, with rock cuttings uphill and guard rails at the drops. Climbs are raised slabs that shadow the hill and show their side (thicker the higher, brighter in the sun), with chevrons up steep stretches (closer the steeper), edge rumble strips that bunch as it steepens, and a bright band at each crest. Higher up, your kart looks a touch bigger and its shadow falls further; it leans into slopes; the camera closes in on a slow climb and pulls out downhill; the minimap's road shades from valley green to summit white. Climbing, you gain speed slower and top out lower, kicking up gravel on the gas. Downhill you speed up, well past your top speed on a long descent (wind streams in from the screen's edges), and lifting slows you less. So lift later uphill (the hill brakes for you) and earlier downhill (you arrive faster and stop slower). The **Valley Straight** (the start, over a stone bridge across the gorge, by a lake and the cable car's valley station) into the **Meadow Sweeper**, a long flat-out left, the Foothills to the Foothill Bend and **the Switchbacks**: four different hairpins stacked up the mountain. A plain right; a left-right jag up the second leg; a left that tightens; a right that opens out; and the tightest, a left onto the Ridge. Along the snowy **Ridge** over the summit (an observatory, the cable car's top station, a waterfall below) to a **long split**, painted as two lanes. Flat out in the white lane takes **the Ramp**, the long way down: a fast dab, the **Cornice** (a left you lift a touch for), then one long, steep straight down the mountain, the map's fastest road, with **the Jog** two thirds down: a real kink to lift for at full speed. Lift into the orange lane for **the Cliff**: a kink off the Ridge, a run-up off the edge, and you **fly down the cliff face** for over two seconds, landing by your speed at the lip. Too slow and you land short in the **torrent** under the jump (it springs from the snow by the Cliff's turn-off, winds down beside the run-up, falls over the cliff just left of the kicker and runs on under your flight): a big splash, under, back up, and an eddy turns you into the current. The wide white-water river carries you down the middle of the map, bobbing, rolling, ducking under in the rapids, knocking off boulders, for about 14 seconds out of your control (the camera rides along), into a calm pool beside the Valley Straight: you bump aground, roll up its sandy beach dripping (past a jetty with a rowboat), shake off and rejoin just short of the line: 6.5-7 s worse than a clean flight. Too fast and you overshoot the marked landing slope, crash on the road and skid off the corner's outside, across the Ramp's last stretch into the **spill**, a white-water stream down the Schuss's west side: a splash, an eddy, a short fast ride onto a little beach further down: about 3.6 s worse than a clean flight (it used to be a plain crash, about 1.3 s). In between you land on the long landing slope (green lines, a red line at its end): slow end of the window at the top, fast end near the bottom. Off the kicker, hang-glider wings in the kart's paint pop out (nose up), vapour off the tips; it rises and grows as it soars, rocking, its shadow sliding down the rocks, then folds them and lands nose down in a burst of dust (short or long, the wings just flap and crumple). Approaching the lip a gauge over your kart shows the window (red short, green good, orange long) with a needle at your speed: lift into the green (fast karts must; slow ones hold it flat); in the air a ring marks your landing. A clean Cliff beats the Ramp by 0.7-1.8 s (1.75 s for Gopher, 0.7 s for Docky: the Ramp is all top speed); the run-up is a bad place to be hit. Both meet atop **the Schuss**, the lap's steepest, fastest descent, into **the Kehre**, a left hairpin at its foot (braking downhill: lift early), then back up beside it into **the Avalanche** (a right that tightens) onto the Valley Straight. Bots brake for the hills, take the Cliff more the better they are (about half to three quarters of the time), aim a little over the window's middle since short costs much more than long (weaker ones less precisely, missing now and then), and never boost toward the lip. A single item box on the outside of the second Switchback hairpin, the left that tightens: carry speed through it.
- **Race Condition**: a steampunk clocktower with real heights, about 35 s a lap. From the cobbled **Plaza** at its foot, the **Escapement** (a left hairpin) takes you in through the gate to climb wooden floors between turning brass gears: the **Lower Gallery** climbing west, the **Ratchet** (a right tightening into a hairpin), the **Upper Gallery** climbing east, and the **Mainspring**, a 360° left coil round the tower's great blue-steel spring, climbing over its own way in on a bridge. Up the **East Face** (a boost pad) onto the top and the **Dial**: a fast 120° balcony sweeper round the great clock face. The clock runs haywire, backwards and fast: its **minute hand** (a brass spade at one end, counterweight at the other) comes round your way, a little slower than you, about every 3.5 s. Each end drops over the arch atop the East Face and sweeps across the balcony to the far arch, dragging sparks (lifting clear past the arches). There's no lane under it: it's a race for the balcony. Beat a blade over the arch and it never catches you; arrive with it and it swats you; follow too close and you hit its back before it's off the balcony, so let it get about a second ahead. Swatted, you're knocked toward the railing and nearly stopped: about 2 s (a Firewall saves you). Then down the **Gallery** outside, high over the roofs on iron columns: the **Cog Esses** (a right-left), the **Belfry Turn** and the **Pendulum Drop**, a steep straight down the west face where the great pendulum swings across the road (its swing hatched in yellow and black). Go flat out between swings or lift and pass behind; hit it and you're knocked spinning, nearly stopped: about 1.7 s. Not watching, you meet the hand on about 1 pass in 4 and the bob on about 2 in 5. It ends in the **Foot**, the lap's slowest corner after the fastest straight, onto the Plaza. Every 15 s **the tower strikes** (a great bell): for 2.5 s every boost pad fires double (longer, a bigger kick: an extra 0.1-0.2 s on the Plaza, nothing straight into a corner). Bots read the hand and the bob (beating a blade over the arch or letting it get ahead, timing the swing) and don't boost into either. The music is a clockwork march: ticking woodblocks and a winding ratchet.
- **Neon Overflow**: a rainy cyber-city at night, about 35 s a lap alone (38 in the wet). Neon city blocks: dark rooftops with air conditioners, skylights, gardens, helipads and billboards, lit windows and blade signs facing the street, neon totems on the pavements, holograms over the plazas, steaming vents, a monorail over the rooftops, the Spire (a tower lit like a crown) inside the Cloverleaf, and a full car park under the Corkscrew. **Rain comes and goes**, each race its own weather (some start wet), with thunder, a flash and a rain sign as each spell arrives. Wet, the road turns to a dark mirror (sign colours smear, karts throw spray) and **every corner grips less** (up to a fifth less when soaked), so lift earlier; once it stops the road dries over half a minute or so. Karts wipe the water off where they drive: a **dry line**, a darker matte groove, forms along the racing line and grips much better, while off it stays wet, so leaving it for a box or a pass costs grip (in steady rain, about a second a lap by lap 3; heavy rain washes it back). Water pools in the dips as **puddles**, deeper the wetter it is: too fast through one and you **aquaplane**: for half a second your tyres float, nothing steers, the tail steps out and you slide wide, usually off the road. Under the speed you cross safely (just over is a twitch, flat out a proper slide). Drifting in the wet slides further, charges the mini-turbo faster and spins you out sooner. The lap is a figure of eight on three levels: **Neon Boulevard** (the wide start) to **Plaza Corner**, a U-turn hairpin after the fastest stretch, **the Ramp** (a right) and up **the Cloverleaf**, a three-quarter loop that **tightens as it climbs** (arrive hot and it catches you at the top), out over its own on-ramp onto **the freeway**, high on pillars: **the Merge** (a kink where a slip road joins, flat out), **the Overpass Sweeper** (a long fast left: a dab, or carry speed wide onto the box at its exit), then down over the Boulevard to **the Storm Drain**, a fast right in the dip at the foot of the off-ramp with the first puddle (you arrive above your top speed), the diagonal to **the Gutter** (a right onto Garage Street) and **the Corkscrew**: a multi-storey car park climbed in a coil, **two turns over itself**, each a fast sweep up the open side and a hairpin loop at the back, the second tighter, levels painted on the ramps. Out on the roof at **the Roof Corner**, along **the Rooftop** and over **the Gap**, a ramp across the alley: at about four fifths of your top speed or more you fly it; slower (or bubbled, or spun on the way) and you **drop into the alley** and restart well back (let go at the lip for a trick). Then down **the Fire Escape** and through **the Sump**, a fast S in the dip under the freeway (the second puddle: the hill hands you more speed than the S wants) onto the Boulevard. Bots plan corners for the grip they expect on their line (weaker ones underrate the wet, arrive hot and have to lift), lift for puddles more the better they are (about half the time to nearly always), and don't boost into one.
- **Gateway 8080**: a busy, sunny airport, about 35 s a lap. Jets in airline tails taxi and take off all round the road, one every 9-14 s (each race has its own timetable), past a glass terminal with jets at its gates, a control tower, a hangar and aprons of tugs pulling luggage carts. The **Runway Straight** (the start, the longest) runs beside the runway, where a jet runs its engines up and rolls the other way, its blast (heat shimmer, streaks rushing back) blowing down the straight. Be just behind it on the gas as it goes (end of run-up, first seconds of roll) and it **slingshots** you way past top speed (wind streaks; a tailwind sign warns one's coming), worth 0.6-0.8 s down the straight. You can still lift, and must for the **Blast Fence turn** at its end, a big lift (harder after a slingshot). Then north up the **Cargo Road** across taxiway Charlie, round **Hangar Corner** to the **Bravo Chicane** across taxiway Bravo. At taxiway crossings red lights flash while a jet's coming: its fuselage and gear are solid (the wings pass over), and hitting one bowls you over for about 3 s, so beat it or wait (about 1-3 s); once past, its blast trails across the road and briefly shoves you sideways (a heavy kart less), onto the kerb if you're near it (about 0.3-0.7 s right behind it, less a little later). Into the **glass terminal**, past check-in islands, shops and seats: **moving walkways** add their speed to yours, and your line leans onto them: one along Check-in outside the Carousel, the **Carousel** itself (a round right hairpin) with the baggage belt on its inside lane (lift early and ride round with the luggage), and one into the fork. At the fork, gas on for **the Concourse**, the long way round the Gate Hairpin, or lift into **the Travelator**, the short way, its walkway running the wrong way (red chevrons): about 0.6-0.7 s slower than the Concourse, unless you're boosting and skim up it (a Turbo fired up it is worth about 1.6-1.8 s there against about 0.8 elsewhere, so about a second quicker than the Concourse). Out past the gates, down the West Road, through the **tunnel** under the runway (jets roll over you, the runway hides you), and a kink onto the straight. Bots wait for a jet at a crossing or beat it if there's time, queue behind a waiting kart, go through a jet's blast, never boost at a jet, and take the Travelator when holding a Turbo for it.
- **Gradient Descent**: a ski run from the summit to the valley lodge, one long run down a snowy mountain, not a loop (the lap counter counts stages), about 1 min 40 s for the winner. Pine forests either side, piste poles on the edges, a chairlift up the mountainside. On snow you **can't really slow down**: lifting bleeds speed at under half the usual rate, gravity keeps pulling, only the air holds you back, so lift early. From the start hut: the Summit Schuss, the Corduroy (three carving turns, each tighter), then the **Pine Glade**: a snowfield six road-widths wide round one long sweeping bend, pine clumps all over it with lanes between, as thick outside as inside, trees along both edges: no way round, every line dodges. Too fast to hold the bend, off the drift you're swept out (lifting barely helps); holding the **drift** carves you in, more the longer you hold. Nothing steers for you: where you are is your drift against the sweep. Your coloured dotted line (big arrowhead at its end) shows where you're heading, a fainter one where pressing or releasing the drift would take you, the drift's marked with the drift button's curved arrow (just after a press or release, while the two are close, the other fades right down so they never overlap). The inside is shorter (hold the drift to stay there): tap in to dodge a clump, let go to drift out past the next. The outside is about a second longer and just as wooded, so you weave it too. Trees are solid: hit one and you crash (a spin, most of your speed gone); still spinning, the next only holds you off, so one crash never bounces you through a clump. A long tree-lined funnel brings every line smoothly back to the piste for Hut Corner. With drift off, lifting carves you in instead, at half a lift's usual cost on snow (in the Black Forest too). Then the traverse and the first **split**: the snow before it painted in halves, the Park's orange with the drift button's arrow, the piste's blue with a plain arrow straight on, a row of flags the commit line. **Hold drift as you cross it and you go into the Park; don't, and you stay on the piste.** Your dotted line shows both ways, the one you're set for bright (with drift off, lift into the Park). **The Park**: a funbox, a box to slide (hold drift along it), the **halfpipe** (you ride wall to wall, airing off each) and a big kicker. Let go of the gas right at each lip for a trick: you're popped on and carried in the flow (the air doesn't hold you back), each trick in a row pops harder, the box counts too; a miss scrubs speed and restarts the chain. In the halfpipe, **pump**: drift the way you're swinging down each wall (the arrow on the snow ahead shows which, bright when right) to gain speed; without, the walls scrub you. A green tick per clean trick, a red cross per miss. Clean, the Park is about 1.5 s up on the piste; with no tricks, almost a second down. Then the **Big Air**: a steep inrun between bunting-strung banks, a sculpted kicker (red dye line at the lip, a flag each end), and a long flight at any speed over the knoll and its spruce branches onto a landing hill dyed with distance lines. Up the inrun the drift key (or touch button) pops up by your kart and on the snow, with a ring of three, pulsing faster near the lip: in the air each press (with drift off, the gas) turns you a third of a flip (side, roof, wheels again), the ring filling a third a press, a star per full flip. Land on a whole number of flips (3, 6, 9... presses) for a boost off the landing, more flips faster and longer (one about 0.6 s, eight about 2 s); land on a third, on your side or roof, and you crash. So mash, count, and stop on a multiple of three: on landing, a green tick and a gold star per flip, or a red cross. A long straight outrun lets a big boost get used. **The Ledge**, the second split, works the same: drift across the flags for the inside lane (its half slate grey, black diamonds), don't to stay on the piste. It's a shelf barely kart-wide across a cliff: rock and boulders up to the piste one side, a sheer drop the other, the valley and wisps of cloud far below. Shorter than the piste round the outside, but lift a touch round the spurs and don't hit the wall fast; off the edge you fall and climb back onto the piste further up from a standstill. The wide piste has a **kicker line**: three kickers across it; let go of the gas at each lip for a trick and a pop of speed, land one without and you scrub. All three: as quick as a careful Ledge; missed, slower. Then **the Slalom**: a long straight of sixteen red and blue poles, drifting left, right, left (drift the way you're swinging to carve; an arrow ahead and the drift button's mark show which way a drift would carve now, bright when that's your swing), in a changing rhythm: steady, a tight pair, a long wide delay gate, an offset pair, a wide one, steady to the end (clip a pole and it knocks you, go inside one and you've missed the gate); the Elbow (a fast left that tightens); **the Moguls** (a long field of bumps round a long right, in even staggered rows like a real mogul field: no lane runs straight down, so you weave with the drift, round a bump in one row, between two in the next. Hit one fast and it launches you, shoved hard off its side, tumbling, a big share of your speed gone; let go of the gas just before one and your legs soften it, but it still costs. Slowly they're smooth but slow; flat out straight down is slowest of all) and **the Black Forest**: a snowfield round a bend the other way, trees edge to edge, no way round. Hold the drift to carve in, lift to hold your line, let go to drift out, and thread the gaps (never drifting runs you into one edge's trees, always drifting into the other's). A long funnel back to the piste, then the Last Schuss through the finish arch to the lodge.

**Front-runner rating.** Some maps suit whoever is in front more than others. Each track's **How it plays** on the map (and its Show more in an online room) has a 0-10 **Front-runner** meter (10: the leader is very hard to catch) with the reason in a line; the map's **Front-runner ratings** switch shows every track's at once and can sort by it. It's measured: 96 bot races with items per track (how often the kart leading at a third, half and two thirds of the race wins, and how often the lead changes), plus what a leader can do on purpose that bots don't: drop a mine where it hurts most (before a jump, the lava, a train) and fire a Turbo where it gains most (a secret shortcut). Moonshot (8.1), Duke's Volcano, Pipeline Pass and Ruby Ruins (6.5-6.6) favour the leader most; Pharaoh's Jackpot (1.4), Race Condition, Recursion Ridge and Kernel Pretzel (2.1-2.4) least, mostly because their boxes keep the leader under fire. Scores within about 1.5 of each other are too close to call.

The big tracks use a camera that follows the human players, zooming out to keep everyone in shot on one shared screen. A corner minimap shows the whole lap. Online, every screen frames every player the same way, yours first.

The HUD has a card per player (mascot, place, lap, speed, held item), the standings top right, and an **event feed** bottom middle: no words, up to four icon chips, the newest sliding in from the right, older ones moving left and fading after a few seconds. A hit reads left to right: who fired, the item, whom it hit (e.g. Gopher, duck, Pyth). Your laps show as a green flag with the lap number, the last lap a white flag; a finish a gold cup for the winner, a medal with the place for a player. Hazards ahead get a yellow sign (train, wormhole with a green or red light for open or shut, logic gate likewise, a hurricane coming in, its wind on the road ahead and which way it'll push you, eruption, low gravity, presses, slag, banking, drawbridge, the rocket's launch pad, fork, a new route), whatever caught you a red one. Chips about your kart have a rim in your colour. Bots' fights among themselves stay out of it.

**Results.** When every player is over the line the results come up over the race, which carries on behind, karts still out filling in their times. The winner stands big on a turntable in their colour ("You win!" when it's you), with your players' places and gaps to the winner, and any new personal best on the track (record time or best lap) in gold. Beside it the finishing order: place, racer, tag (YOU, P1-P4, or online the player's name, in their colour; bots quieter), time, gap and best lap, your rows outlined in your colour. **RACE AGAIN** (`Enter`) reruns the race, the next track's button (`N`) goes on, **Menu** (top left, `Esc` or `M`) goes back, `C` opens What's new. It fits a phone either way up and an iPad; the rows stagger in quickly, not at all with Reduced effects or your system's reduced motion.

## Cup mode
Pick **Cup** on the title, then on the map a ready-made cup or **Your own cup**: click 3 to 5 tracks in race order to fill its numbered slots (the track cards get numbered badges); click a track again, or its slot's ✕, to remove it. Your custom cup and your chosen cup are remembered (choosing a ready-made one keeps yours; clicking a track goes back to it). Ready-made cup cards show a trophy and the tracks in order; set the laps and bots and press **START CUP**: **Coast** (Merge Bay, Async Atoll, Sakura Stack, Crab Harbor), **Sun** (Cron Canyon, Ruby Ruins, Pharaoh's Jackpot, Pyth Plateau), **Magma** (Assembly Line, Kernel Pretzel, Duke's Volcano), **Peak** (Pipeline Pass, Cryo Caldera, Recursion Ridge, Heap Space) and **City** (Gateway 8080, Race Condition, Neon Overflow, from the airport by day to the streets at night in the rain): every track but the giant finale in exactly one, each building to its showpiece; plus the **Kernel** cup, the grand final: three of the best, then the giant one-lap finale and the ski run down to the lodge (Crab Harbor, Pyth Plateau, Duke's Volcano, Moonshot, Gradient Descent).

The races run back to back with the same racers, laps and bot skill. Every kart, human or bot, scores by finishing place: 10/7/5/3 with 4 karts (10/6/3 with 3, 10/5 with 2). Ties go to more wins, then the better last race. From the second race the grid starts in reverse standings order, so the leader starts at the back. The loading screen shows the cup so far (done tracks ticked, this one lit) and who leads. The results show the winner, the race with each place's points (+10, +7...) and the cup standings, which play out quickly: points fly from each result into its total, then the rows slide into the new order with places moved (▲2, ▼1) and each one's places so far. Dots at the top show progress (done, this one, to come), the footer the next track's picture and races left. `Enter`, `Space` or a click skips to the final table, then `Enter` goes on: **Next race** instead of a rematch, and after the last race a podium (the top three in 3D on the steps, the champion in the middle hopping for joy, confetti) with the final table and the cup's tracks; **Race this cup again** (`R`) or **Menu**. `Esc` (or Quit cup, top left) abandons the cup for the menu. With Reduced effects or your system's reduced motion, the standings show their final state at once.

Cups work online too: the host switches the room's Track panel to **Cup**, anyone in the room picks a ready-made cup or builds one (each track tagged with who added it), and the host presses **START CUP**. Everyone sees the same standings after each race (players by name, bots by racer), the host presses **Next race** (or `Enter`) for everyone, and everyone gets the podium. A player who leaves mid-cup keeps their points, marked "left", and a bot drives their kart from then on; someone joining mid-cup races once it's over. **End cup** (or `Esc` mid-race) takes everyone back to the room.

## Time trial
Pick **Time trial** on the title, your racer, then a track, and press **START TRIAL**. It's you (P1's racer) alone: no bots, item boxes or bits, and **no obstacles**: nothing comes at you or shuts the road, so every lap is down to you. No trains on Cron Canyon (gates up), the Garbage Collector bots and the Moon's rovers parked on the verge, no lava from Duke's Volcano or Kernel Pretzel (its globs and the lava river's surges), no boulders on Ruby Ruins, the crab asleep on its beach, no waves or hurricanes, the jets at their gates (no taxiing, no blast), the presses parked, the slag never poured, the drawbridge down, the clock stopped (minute hand up over the arches, pendulum hooked back off the road), the logic gate held open, Kernel Pretzel's thicket a charred gap that stays open, and on Moonshot the gantry arms back, the test stand quiet, the crawler and rover waiting at the end of their tracks, the airlocks open. The road and all that's part of it stays: jumps and ramps, the cliffs and their windows, banks, forks and splits, walls, belts, pads (and the clocktower's strike doubling them), the ski run, the Rollers, the wormholes and their cycle, the mass driver, the rocket rows, the secret shortcuts, and Neon Overflow's rain (the same every attempt). **Only your fastest lap counts**: on a circuit it's hot laps from the grid, as many as you like (`Esc` or the pause button ends it), each timed, your track time your best single lap, never a total. Where a lap alone means nothing, the whole run is the time: on Pyth Plateau, whose road changes every lap, its 3 roads; on Pipeline Pass and Gradient Descent the whole way down; Moonshot is always one grand lap. (Records were always the best single lap on a circuit, so old ones carry over.)

Your best is big and gold at the top (**BEST LAP**), with a live **VS BEST** beside it: how far ahead (green, −) or behind (red, +) your best lap you are now, from where your ghost was here. The HUD card has the clock, the lap and your last lap; the right panel lists this session's laps (fastest in gold). Your best lap is saved per track with its **ghost**: a see-through, pale blue copy of your kart and mascot driving that lap again on every later one from the line, so you see exactly where you gain or lose; it's on the minimap too. A split pops up under the target every quarter lap, and at the line for the whole lap. Beat your best and the clock turns gold with **NEW BEST** on the card, a gold burst and a chime, and that lap is the ghost from the next one.

`R` restarts from the grid, `Esc` (or **End trial** on the pause card) ends the trial. The results show your racer on its turntable with this time's fastest lap, big, against your best (a new best says by how much) and any friend's ghost you raced; that lap's **sectors** (its four quarters; a run's laps or stages) against your old best's and the friend's, with the ideal lap from your session's best sectors; then every lap (fastest gold, a new best marked). **RETRY** (`Enter` or `R`), the next track's button (`N`), **Share ghost** (`S`, copies your ghost's link), **Menu** (top left, `Esc`). In Time trial mode track cards carry your best time, and the map's left side shows it for the picked track. Records and ghosts with drift on are kept apart from those with it off (the trial's box, loading screen and results say which), so you never race a ghost from the other game. Times from before time trials lost their obstacles are kept apart too (nothing is deleted): not raced against, and until you set a new time on a track its box shows the old one ("with obstacles it was 0:36.42"). Records and ghosts stay in this browser (a ghost is ~3-12 KB; if storage fills, the oldest other tracks' ghosts go first, never their times). Time trial is offline only: the online room has no such mode (but shows everyone's bests, below). It remembers its own track, apart from the single race's.

**Friends' ghosts.** **Share my ghost** on the time trial box (or **Share ghost** on the results, `S`) copies a link packing in your best ghost on that track: track, racer, time, drift on or off and the whole lap, about 1,000-1,300 characters for a 30-40 s lap (about 25-35 a second; Moonshot and Epoch Expressway's grand laps about 3,000). The first time, it asks your name (the online room's). The link opens the web version (or a LAN server's address) on the menu in Time trial on that track with a card, "Racing Pia's ghost: 0:31.42", and **RACE IT**. The friend's ghost drives in pink with their name under it, beside your pale blue one; the target shows your best, theirs (in pink) and the live gap **VS PIA**, and the splits are against them too. Beat it and the results offer **Send yours back to Pia**. A link's drift setting switches yours to match. Friends' ghosts are kept in this browser per track (six at most, one per friend: a newer one replaces theirs), listed as **Rivals** on the time trial box: click one to race it (again to race only your own ghost), ✕ to forget it. A link from a since-redone track layout, from before time trials lost their obstacles (its ghost waited for trains and lava: ask for a new one), or cut off, says so instead.

**The leaderboard.** Everyone's best time on each track, kept on the game's own server (whoever runs `npm run play` or `npm run server`, the one online rooms use), so friends see each other's times from anywhere that reaches it, not only in the same room. It's on the time trial box and the results: rank, name, racer, time and gap to the fastest, top 20 per track (one time per name; your row marked), drift and no drift apart like your records. **Race** on a row fetches that ghost as your rival there, as a ghost link does (on the results, straight into a retry against it). A new best is posted by itself under your name (the online room's and ghost links'; with none yet, the results ask once); **Post my best** posts one not there yet. The server keeps the boards in `.kk-board.json` next to it (`npm run play` in the main checkout; `KK_BOARD_FILE=<path>` puts it elsewhere) and checks what comes in: a sane time, a short name, and a ghost that opens and really is that time on that track. The web version has no server, so no leaderboard, unless opened with `?server=<address>` (remembered; empty `?server=` forgets it) pointing at a reachable game server (https, for an https page).

**In an online room**, the room's track (a single race on a picked track) shows **Time trial bests**: everyone's best time trial lap there with the room's drift setting, fastest first, with who set it on what racer. They're swapped automatically when someone joins and when the track or drift changes (each player's best and ghost go once, never during a race). **Save ghost** keeps a friend's ghost as one of your rivals on that track, as a link would, for Time trial later.

## Fight mode
No karts: two mascots in a walled arena, 1v1. Pick **Fight** on the title (the fifth tile), set P1 and P2 to a player or a bot (Chill, Normal, Ruthless), pick fighters and press **FIGHT**. All ten racers fight (**Tux**, **Duke**, **Gopher**, **Ferris**, **Pyth**, **Octo**, **Docky**, **Swifty**, **Cpp** and **Bugdroid**), each rebuilt around three big moves and an evade of their own. Fighters still being reworked are greyed out with a small WIP tag, but pickable. The setup is a fighting game's select screen: each side's fighter big on its own spotlit stage in its side's colour, with its name plate, showing off its moves; the roster in the middle with each side's cursor (**P1** red, **P2** blue), a big VS over it, the maps under it. Move your cursor with your keys (two on one keyboard: P1 `WASD`, P2 the arrows; one player: either), or click a fighter to pick for the side whose card you last clicked (P1 at first), right-click for the other. On a touch screen (or narrow window) a **P1 / P2** switch under the roster says which side a tap picks for; an upright iPad shows the cards side by side. A picked fighter spins in with a hop and a cheer. Each stage shows at a glance how good it is: a five-step **Strength** meter (from how often it wins bot fights against everyone at Normal), how hard it is to play well (**Easy**, **Medium** or **Hard**) and its playstyle on the name plate (Ice tank, Assassin, Zoner ...). Each card has three tabs: **Fighter** (who it is in a line; five bars from bot fights, compared across the ten: **Damage** dealt a round, **Speed** on foot, **Range** its hits land from, **Defense** (how little damage it takes), **Ease**; and **Strong vs** / **Weak vs**: best and worst matchups with heads and win %), **Moves** (what it's like and its passive, then the three moves and evade: that player's key, icon and name, and whether holding the key aims or steers; click one for a big card with a live clip, what it does, its **tell** (how you see it coming) and **answer** (how to beat it), `←` `→` for the others) and **Keys** (that player's keys drawn as on the keyboard, with each move's name). Under the VS, big, the matchup: each name and share of fights won ("Tux 56% · 44% Duke") over a split bar (P1 red, P2 blue), a word for it (Even, Slight edge, Favoured, Hard counter) and its source (bots against each other at the bot side's level, Normal between two players, how many fights; marked "measured on an earlier version" if a fighter has changed since); while you pick, each tile shows its share against the other side's fighter. **Random** rolls a fighter you see coming: it stands on that side's stage marked Random (next up) with its matchup, **FIGHT** uses exactly it, and the next is rolled after (Rematch rolls fresh). Your picks are remembered.

**Rounds and wins.** Before each round the portraits slam in from the sides on bands in their colours, VS flashes where they meet with the round number over it, both cheer and get ready in their own pose (Tux crouched for his slide, Duke with a hand up to snap), then 3, 2, 1 drum in and an impact star starts it. A knock-out freezes the picture a moment (a flash, lines closing in on the one going down) and the camera eases in; then a spotlight falls on the round's winner (a hop, confetti), a score card rises under them with both faces, the score, the stars and the next round, and the picture dips to dark into it (a double KO is a draw: both dizzy, nobody's star). For the match winner the camera pushes in on their victory hops, their colour washing in at the edges, streamers; then the **results** fill the screen: the winner cheering big on their stage on the left (the loser small and dazed behind), on the right who won and how, the score with faces and stars, each round, the head-to-head numbers (damage, hits, counters, knockdowns, wall slams) and **Rematch** (`Enter`), **Swap fighters**, **Change fighters** (`Esc`), **Menu**; upright on a phone the stage is on top. Online the host gets Rematch / Back to room and players wait.

**Maps.** Pick one on the setup screen (a card each with a little top-down picture): **Sky Ring** (the floating stone ring: open ground, a low wall all round), **Pillar Court** (a square yard with four stone pillars: they block walking, knockback bounces off them like the wall, and you can hide behind one), **Data Bus** (a long narrow hall: no circling round, two crates by the walls, and the side lanes run like conveyor belts, one each way) and **Cold Boot** (an ice rink round a hot core: ice in the middle where you slide, and two heat vents that glow, then flare every 7 s and knock anyone on them down with a bit of damage).

**Winning.** By default first to 2 rounds won, 60 s a round; the select screen (and an online room's host) can set first to 1, 2, 3 or 5, and 40, 60 or 90 s. Health only: win a round by knocking the other's health to zero (a KO); nobody can be knocked out of the arena. Its edge is a low solid wall: knocked into it (or a pillar or crate) hard enough, a fighter slams off it, takes a little extra damage and goes down. When the clock runs out it's sudden death: every hit does more damage, ×1.5 rising to ×3 over 20 s (the clock shows it), until someone goes down.

**Knocked down.** A big hit, a full charge or a wall slam floors you, but only for a moment (about 0.4 s). While you're down you can still be hit, but lighter (half damage, a short slide, a little dust and a dull thump instead of the big impact), never longer or floored again, and at most two hits each time you go down. Grabs can't pick you up off the floor. Press any key a moment after you land to **roll up** at once: hold a direction to roll that way (nothing held, away from them). You can't be hit while rolling, and rolling up with the evade key doesn't use your evade. Back on your feet you're safe a moment: a hit staggers you instead of flooring you again. Bots hit you on the floor when close enough and roll up in their own ways (below).

**The bots.** Each level plays like a different kind of player, not just a quicker one. **Chill** is friendly and easy to read: it leans on a favourite move, often squares up a moment before a big one (a quick jab with you right in front of it mostly just comes out), takes a breather now and then (backing off to let you recover, often after landing a hit), forgets its evade, doesn't chase hard (a close-up fighter a bit harder, since it has to reach you), rarely hits you on the floor and always gets up the same way. **Normal** is balanced. **Ruthless** adapts: it answers what you keep doing (sees your favourite move coming and steps out of it or beats you to it; hovers just outside its range and punishes the miss, if it's close or mid-range, not a long-range one, where a zoner wants it), baits a habitual evade with a quick feint and punishes it, punishes a missed move nearly every time, mixes up its own moves, plays its fighter's setups (Pyth's venom then the Coil, Ferris's Lock then the Claw, Docky's wave then Compose Up then the slap, Duke's Stop the World then jabs and the pot), and on the floor reads your roll: stands where you usually roll and meets you as you're up, or gets up the way you're not covering, sometimes staying down. Every bot also has a mild style (aggressive, patient or tricky), so two Normal bots don't play alike, plus its fighter's character: Duke presses in, Swifty hits and runs, Docky waits for the opening and makes it count, Octo keeps you at tentacle's length, Cpp and Bugdroid set traps before engaging, Gopher is tricky.

**Three moves and an evade.** No jabs, no shield, no stamina: each fighter has three big moves, one key each, and an evade on the fourth (`Space` for one player). Each has a cooldown: its key, and its icon on the fighter's card at the top, sweeps back round as it returns. The keys mean the same for everyone, listed in this order below: the first (`J`) the basic move, quick and back soonest; the second (`K`) a secondary with a bit longer cooldown; the third (`L`) the most special ability, longest to come back, so it can't be spammed. They're big and a little "broken" on purpose. Most show they're coming, softly, on the floor (a shadow, a grey ripple, a coffee stain, a warm glow, an ink blot): your cue to walk out or evade. Most fighters also have a **hit**: a big, quick, whole-body blow with no floor mark, out almost at once (Tux's belly slide, Gopher's Dig & Pop, Docky's tail slap...); its tell is the body winding up for a blink, its answer spacing: its reach is short. Every move leaves its user open when it misses. Aiming is gentle: an aimed move (Ferris's claw, say) starts on the other fighter and, stick left alone, follows them a little, so doing nothing is a good aim; hold a direction to move it off them (to lead a runner). Bots aim a little worse, the easier the worse. They play like people too: they miss some tells, go in on their own rhythm instead of only waiting for you, sometimes press a move just as you press yours (so you trade, or catch them in it), and spend their evade getting round you, so it isn't always ready; the easier, the more reckless. Presses are remembered a moment and come out as soon as you can act. Hit someone winding up for a **counter** (more damage, a big sharp star). Big hits and hard wall slams **knock them down**: they fall flat (a hit while they're down lands lighter, see Knocked down), scramble up (untouchable a moment), then flicker softly a moment: a hit only staggers them, and they can't be knocked down, wall-slammed or grabbed (ending early if they swing first), so no getting knocked straight back down as you stand. The icon feed at the bottom shows counters, knockdowns, KOs and a Stop the World catching someone.

Step off the line: it turns slowly and stops turning before it fires). **Background Service** (his trap, not a hit: he flicks a little app tile, green with a white robot head, across the floor to where you stand. It skids there, beds in with a puff of dust and arms, blinking faster and faster, then goes faint, running quietly in the background with a slow soft blink, for a few seconds. Step on it and jagged bolts crackle up into you: a little damage and locked up a moment with bolts crawling over you, just long enough for his beam. Two at a time. Flicked at someone right on him, he hops back off them. Step off it before it arms, then walk round it). **Sideload** (green motes stream into his cupped hands and an app cube pops into being; he crouches, sets it down and pushes it out, it hops into the floor ahead and plugs in, a ring of light filling on top while it boots; then for a few seconds it turns its lens to you and pops slow glowing pellets, each a small staggering hit. Sidestep them or keep a pillar between you. Walk up to it and it zaps you away with a crackle; it then needs a moment to recharge, so walk into it again and you kick it over. One cube at a time). Evade **Power Surge** (rooted, arms out: sparks gather in for a blink (hit him then and it lands), then lightning crackles round him a moment. A hit into it is soaked with a bright crack, and if it came from close by (a swing, a grab, a dash) bolts leap onto you, locking you up a moment; a shot from afar just fizzles. Soaking one, he's quickly free; if nothing comes, a last little crackle shoves anyone right next to him off, then the arcs sputter out and he's left drained and rooted a moment, open. Don't swing into the lightning: wait a step back and hit him as it fizzles. It comes back slowly).

**Practice.** **Practice**, next to FIGHT on the select screen: P1's fighter (you) against P2's pick as a training dummy, on the map you picked. No rounds, clock or KO: health refills a moment after a combo ends (knocked to zero, a fighter just goes down and gets up full), and `R` (or **Reset**) puts you both back at a round's start. The dummy's modes, keys `1`-`5` or the buttons on the right: **Stand** (still), **Walk** (circles you, turning now and then), **Evade** (evades about half your moves as their tell comes out: bait it, then punish), **Fight back** (a Normal bot), **Replay** (press `T` to play the dummy for 5 s while your fighter waits; it then repeats that, to practise against your own attack). On screen: your moves on the left (key, icon, damage, startup, active and recovery frames at 60 a second, the live cooldown, its card's first line); on the right the dummy, your **last move**'s frames as it actually ran and what it left you with (on a hit, how many frames you act before or after them; on a miss, whether the dummy's quickest move fits in your recovery: unsafe), the **last hit** (damage, stagger, knockdown, counter hit, hit on the floor), the combo count and damage, and a **checklist** of nine things to learn with any fighter (land each move, a counter hit, a 3-hit combo, hit someone down, a wall slam, punish a recovery, make them miss with your evade), ticked as you go and kept per fighter. Beside it, **Trials**: three or four combos per fighter, built on what really links in their kit. Pick one (or `C` for the next): it says what to do, shows each step's key and icon, sets the dummy to suit and lights each step as you land it; break the string and it says which step went wrong and why (too late, a miss, or not quite: a Coil on someone the poison's worn off). **Watch it** (`V`) plays a little demo. Done ones are ticked and kept with the checklist. The trials: **Tux** Fish row (three slaps, the third the wallop), Slap and slide, Ice plough (armoured slide), Freeze and slide (armoured fish, then the slide); **Duke** Blink and jab, Boil over, Freeze string (Stop the World, Hot Java and a jab on the statue, the pot boils over), Hot potato (blink in, pot on, blink out); **Gopher** Burrow then Pop, Deadlock and Pop, Stuck in the rush, Under the deadlock (Deadlock, Burrow, Pop); **Ferris** Claw into spin, Lock and Claw, The borrow checker (Lock, Claw, spin), Shell burst; **Pyth** Bite then Coil, Bite then Whip, Slither and squeeze, No way out (against an evading dummy: bitten, it can't evade); **Octo** Crossfire (Fork, then a blame from both), Blame, fork, blame, Push to the wall; **Docky** Wave into the slap, Into the box (Compose Up, then the slap into it), Wave, wall, slam; **Swifty** Hop then Catch, Loop and dart, Gust to the wall; **Cpp** Fly and call, The swarm (two flies, then the call), Poisoned swarm (sludge, a fly, the call); **Bugdroid** Lock-up and beam (Background Service, then the beam), Turret and beam, The whole toolbox. `H` hides it all; Esc (or ⏸ by touch) opens the menu: the dummy's modes, Reset, back to the select screen.

**Controls.** Fight plays with a keyboard, a gamepad or a touch screen. **Touch** (iPhone, iPad, landscape or portrait): a thumb down anywhere on the left half puts a thumb stick under it, moving you any direction (drag past its rim and it follows); the four big round buttons bottom right are your three moves and, in the corner, your evade, each with its icon, its cooldown sweeping round and Duke's evade charges. A move that aims or steers (Tux's slide, Ferris's claw, Bugdroid's beam...) follows the stick while you hold its button, one you can charge (Octo's Force Push...) gathers while held, and a thumb sliding onto the next button presses it. A small ⏸ top right pauses. In portrait the arena is framed above the controls. Two players on one iPad each take half the screen, with a smaller stick and buttons. The controls show when you touch the screen and hide when you use keys or a pad; the setup screen's Touch tab and each move's card show which button is which. One player on keys: `WASD` or the arrow keys move, `J` `K` `L` the three moves, `Space` (or `;`) the evade. Two on one keyboard (laid out so two people fit on one laptop, hands well apart): P1 `WASD` move, `F` `G` `H` the moves, `Space` (or left `Shift`) the evade; P2 the same shape one hand over: `O` `K` `L` `;` move, `.` the evade (under them, like P1's `Space`; or right `Shift`), the arrow keys `←` `↓` `→` the moves (or the numpad: `4` `5` `6` the moves, `0` the evade). Gamepads (the first pad is the first player): stick or d-pad, A X B the moves, LB or RB the evade, Start pauses. In a fight each player's card shows their health and move icons with cooldowns; their keys (or pad) sit in the bottom corner, each with its move's icon and name, lighting up when pressed and sweeping round while it comes back. `Esc` or `P` pauses and resumes (the pause card: Resume, Restart `R`, Quit to menu `Q`). The results show who won, the rounds and per-fighter numbers (damage, hits, counters, knockdowns, wall slams), with **Rematch** (`Enter`), **Swap fighters** (the same fight, fighters traded) and **Change fighters** (`Esc`: back to the select screen, picks kept).

**Online.** In an online room the host can switch the mode to **Fight**. The room then shows the setup screen's two big fighter cards side by side, P1 red and P2 blue, VS between: who's on each, their fighter turning on its stage, its stats, passive and moves (click one to watch it). The first two players fight, on keys, a gamepad or touch (an iPhone or iPad plays by touch; one alone fights a bot, whose level and fighter the host picks on its card); you pick your fighter on your card and see the other change as they pick. Everyone else watches, listed under the cards. The host sets **Supers** and presses **START FIGHT**; everyone else sees a "Waiting for the host" chip. After the fight the results say who beat whom on what; the host has **Rematch** and **Back to room**.

## Music
Every screen has its own chiptune, generated from a seed: the menu, the results, and one song per track. The race music speeds up on your final lap. `Backspace` mutes everything.

## Players
Up to 4 humans on one keyboard, three keys each (gas, item, drift), spread out depending on how many play:

| Players | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Q` `W` `E` (or `Space` `Enter` `Shift`) | | | |
| 2 | `Q` `W` `E` | `O` `P` `[` | | |
| 3 | `Q` `W` `E` | `V` `B` `N` | `[` `]` `\` | |
| 4 | `Q` `W` `E` | `C` `V` `B` | `N` `M` `,` | `O` `P` `[` |

**Gamepads** work too: the first four pads plugged in are P1 to P4 (online, the first pad is you). In a race `A` is gas, `B` or the left shoulder or trigger drift, `X` or the right shoulder or trigger the item, `Start` pauses; in the menus, results and pause card the d-pad or left stick moves, `A` presses, `B` goes back and `Start` goes on.

Players are told apart by tag as well as colour (P1-P4 tags over the karts, on the cards and in the standings; with 2+ players the minimap dots carry their numbers, and on a touch screen each item button its player's tag). Each player's keys show in their colour on the racers screen (under their racer, "In the race"), on the loading screen, and online in your own card in the room.

On a touch screen each player gets a zone with an item button, and a pause button sits top right. AI fills the grid to 4. On a phone the HUD (cards, standings, minimap, feed, countdown) is drawn smaller, scaled from the screen's short side, so it doesn't cover the road; the touch buttons stay a thumb's width.
`P` pauses (`Tab` with 2+ players, since `P` is a player key; the race also pauses when the tab is hidden), `Backspace` mutes, `Esc` quits to the menu. The pause card has **Resume**, **Restart race** (`R`: the same grid and track from the start; in a time trial **Start again**) and **Quit to menu** (in a time trial, **End trial**), by tap, click or arrow keys, plus the settings you'd want mid-race: sound, screen shake, reduced effects and drift (a drift change counts from the next race, or a restart), and **Full screen**.
`F` (or the **Full screen** button in the menu's top bar, the room and the pause card) switches full screen where the browser can (not on an iPhone, whose Safari can't; Add to Home Screen does it there). On phones and tablets the screen stays on while you race or wait in a room (where the browser has the Wake Lock API), the race never zooms, scrolls or pops up a menu on tap or hold, and the HUD and touch buttons keep clear of the notch and the home bar.

### Online (same Wi-Fi)
Up to 4 players, each on their own laptop or phone, on the same network:

1. One person starts the game's server: `npm run dev` (or `npm run server`, which builds the game and serves it on port 8080 without the dev tools; `PORT=3000 npm run server` picks another port). Both listen on the local network and print a "Network" address such as `http://192.168.1.23:5173/`.
2. They pick **Online** on the menu's title, type a name and press **Host a room** (it opens with the track, laps and bots of their last one). The room screen shows a four-letter code (no I, L, O or Q, so nothing to misread) and the room's link to send, e.g. `http://192.168.1.23:5173/#join=KRTW` (click it or **Copy**).
3. Everyone else opens that link on the same Wi-Fi (or scans the room's **QR code**: the button beside **Copy** shows the link big for a phone's camera) and is straight in, nothing to press: with their saved name, or the first time a made-up one (Pixel Pete, Async Ace...) that the ✎ beside it changes (remembered after that; the host can change theirs the same way). It works on a phone, on a page already running the game, and mid-race (you wait in the room for the next one). Or they pick **Online** on the menu, type a name and the code and press **Join**. The web copy (GitHub Pages) can't host or join rooms: a room link opened there says so and asks for the host's link (or paste it there and press **Go**). The room is one screen: on the left the code and address, everyone on their own turntable in their colour, the bots and the chat; on the right your racer, the track or cup and the race settings; **START** at the bottom, **Leave room** (the host: **Close room**) top left. Each picks a racer (two or more may share one; each racer card carries everyone's tag on it) and anyone can pick the track, or **Random** for a different one every race (in a cup, a ready-made cup, adding and taking out tracks); the track's card names whoever picked it. Everyone sees the whole room. The host alone sets single race or cup, laps, drift (on or off for everyone; it starts as the host's own Drift setting), bots and bot skill (greyed for everyone else), and presses **START RACE**. To keep it friendly, everyone but the host can change their racer once every 2 seconds and the track (or cup) once every 2 seconds. Your pick shows at once; just after a change that section dims with a ring counting down, and a card you click then shakes and shows how long is left.

Rooms are for one local network: everyone has to be on the same Wi-Fi (or wired network) as the host's machine. `KK_SHARE_IP=<address> npm run play` makes the link use a particular address, if the machine has more than one.

Everyone plays with `Space` (or `Q`) for gas and `Enter` (or `W`) for items, or the touch zone and item button on a phone. Your screen follows your own kart. The host's machine runs the race; the others send only their keys and draw what the host sends (20 times a second, smoothed in between, carried on for a moment when patchy Wi-Fi holds one up; each key press is resent with the next few, so a quick tap isn't lost), so the host should keep the game in view: a hidden tab stops the race for everyone. The host and each player also open a direct browser-to-browser link for the race (WebRTC, on your own network: nothing goes out to the internet), where one lost Wi-Fi packet doesn't hold up the rest; if it can't be made (a firewall, say), the race goes over the game's server. No pause online. **Link quality:** in the room each player's card has a three-bar chip with their round trip to the host (green under 90 ms, amber under 220, red above), showing whose Wi-Fi is struggling; in a race, if your own connection is poor (the host's updates late or uneven), a small red Wi-Fi sign shows at the bottom of your screen, so a jerky race gets put down to the connection. If a player leaves, a bot drives their kart home; if the host leaves, the room closes for everyone. If a player's connection drops (a Wi-Fi blip, an iPad's screen going off), their screen says **Reconnecting…** and retries the same room with the same name for 20 seconds, then puts them back in the room on their racer; a race that's on goes on without them (a bot drives their kart home) and they're in the next one (in a cup, from the next cup). **Leave room** on that card gives up at once. After a race the host takes everyone back to the room; `Esc` does the same for the host mid-race, and leaves the room for anyone else. The host can also run a cup (see Cup mode): races in a row with the standings between, and a podium at the end. After a single race the host's results have **REMATCH** (Enter: the same track again), **Next** (N: the next track, with its picture, or another random one on Random) and **Back to room**, to go again without the room in between; everyone else's say they're waiting for the host, with the chat beside the results.

**Chat.** Everyone in a room can chat, in the room's **Chat** panel under who's racing (and on the results and the podium): type and press `Enter` (or **Send**), or tap a quick reaction (**gg**, **nice!**, **oops**, **rematch?**). Names are in each player's colour. Whoever joins sees the last lines before they came in (the room keeps 50). In a race, new lines show for a few seconds in a small stack on the left under the player cards, then fade; press `T` (or `/`) to type one: while it's open your keys go to the chat only (your gas lets go as it opens, so keep your thumb ready), `Enter` sends and `Esc` closes. On a phone or tablet, tap the chat button above the item button. A soft blip says someone else wrote. A line is at most 120 characters of plain text (no links or formatting), about one every 0.7 seconds. If you've scrolled away from the chat in the room, a new line peeks in at the bottom of the screen.

The first time, macOS or Windows may ask whether Node may accept incoming connections: allow it, or friends can't connect. Playing online needs one of those two servers: a copy of `dist/` on a plain web host plays offline only.

## Racers
Two or more players may pick the same racer, offline or online (the bots still go for racers nobody picked). Each player's kart is then painted in their own colour (P1 red, P2 blue, P3 green, P4 orange) under their P1..P4 tag; their HUD card, standings row, minimap dot, head in the event feed and dot in the results and cup standings take that colour too, and a cup scores each separately. A bot on a racer a player has is a darker, plainer version with a grey dot. A racer of your own looks as it always does.

Every racer drives their own kind of car. Bigger cars are harder to shove but bigger targets and can't hug the inside of a corner as tightly; small ones are nimble but get bumped about. Stats are balanced by simulation: alone, everyone's lap pace is within about ±2% (Docky about 2% quicker: he pays for it with the slowest launch), and in random four-kart races everyone's average finishing place is within about 0.2 of each other (except Cpp, whose stats the user set: all speed, no grip).
