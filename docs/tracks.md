# Kernel Kart: making a track, and every mechanic

## Making a track
Tracks live in `js/tracks.js`. A good Kernel Kart track has three things: **corners that feel different from each other**, **enough length** to have a shape worth learning, and **at least one mechanic that makes you think**. Always try a new mechanic, not just a new layout.

### The one-button rule
The player only controls the throttle; the car steers itself (`README.md` "How it plays"). So every corner and mechanic has to come down to **when to lift, how long, and when to floor it**. A corner is interesting when the right answer differs from the corner before it. A mechanic is interesting when it gives you a throttle decision with a risk on both sides (the train: floor it or wait; the crevasse: flat out or fall in; the fork: lift just enough to tuck in).

### Corner variety
Each layout point is `[x, y, r]`: the corner at that point is rounded with radius `r` (layout units, × `scale`). Roughly:
- `r` 50–70: hairpin, a big lift. 80–110: a normal corner. 120–200: a fast sweeper, a dab or flat. 220+: a long arc or a carousel.

Vary more than the radius:
- **Angle**: 45° kinks, 90°s, 180° hairpins, 270° carousels and pigtails, 360° loops over a bridge (`bridge: 'first'`).
- **Combinations**: chicanes, S-bends, double apexes, staircases, a decreasing-radius corner (a big `r` into a small one) that catches people who commit early.
- **Approach**: the same corner feels different after a long straight than after a slow hairpin. Put the hardest corner at the end of the fastest straight at least once.
- **Width**: `widths: [[x, y, mul, halfLen, ramp?]]` (ramp: how long it takes to widen, default 120 world units) narrows a ledge or a bridge, or opens a wide braking zone. `runoff` sets how forgiving the grass is.
- **Grip**: `gripMul` (ice), or a pad (`pads`) right before a corner as a trap.
- **Item boxes**: rows go in by themselves (`makeBoxes`, geometry.js: on the straightest bit near evenly spaced spots), two side by side. About a third of each track's pair rows should be a single box, picked by hand for the layout (the user: not every third row at random): `singleBoxes: [[x, y, lat], ...]`, a layout point and a share of the half-width (+ is the driver's right); the row nearest the point moves there as one box, with a comment line saying why. A single on a straight is still a sure thing (the box pull in race.js draws every line onto it), so put it where the throttle decides the line: on the outside of a corner's exit or round a carousel's outer wall (a kart carrying speed runs out there, one that lifts early tucks in past it), or on the inside of a fast corner you could take flat (lift a touch and you take it, flat out you sail wide). Keep pairs on the start straight, where roads meet again, and before a big mechanic. Check every single with `npm run boxes`: someone has to take it (none at 0% across the board) and not everyone (a 100% row is no choice). `sim.test.mjs` fails a single that no longer lands near a row (a layout change). A stretch where a row would pull the line off an apex can be kept clear (`noBoxes: [[x, y, half-length]]`, layout units; Neon Overflow's Corkscrew loops, Plaza Corner and the Roof Corner).

Check: if you can describe two corners on the lap the same way ("medium right after a straight"), change one. Scan the lap's `vmaxAt` profile and make sure the corner speeds are spread out, not all the same.

### Length
(Moonshot, the grand final, is one lap of 38.5k (31.2k of it driven): a definition's `laps: 1` sets a track's lap count whatever the setting, offline, online and in a time trial; the menu card says so.) Today's other closed laps are 4.8k–15k world units, about 16–46 s a lap for the winner. New tracks should be on the long side, **about 20–30 s a lap or more** (`L` above 6000). That's long enough for a straight, a technical section, a mechanic and a recovery stretch. Use `scale` (1.4–1.6) and `camera: true` (every track has it now: the user wants the view zoomed in on the players everywhere). Long is not the same as empty: every straight should lead into something.

### Mechanics we already have
Ramps and tricks (`jumps`), cannon jumps over a crevasse (`jumps` + `gaps`; on Neon Overflow an alley between two roofs), boost pads (`pads`), Garbage Collector bots crossing the road (`gc`), a new route every lap (`routes`), forks you choose by how you take a corner (`forks`), a long split (`forks: [{ at, split: true }]`, Kernel Pretzel: see below; a fork's other road is `routes[fs.alt]`, the route whose `bits` (default its index) are this one's plus the fork's bit, so a course needn't list every combination: Kernel Pretzel's Ash Run is `bits: 3` and there's no route 2 = Loop + Ash; a road with no route for a fork never meets it), self-crossing figure-eights (`bridge`; `bridge: 'high'` on a track with heights makes the higher pass the deck at every crossing, Neon Overflow), narrow and wide sections (`widths`), low grip (`gripMul`), point-to-point stage courses (`open`).
Newer ones, each in its own `race-*.js` (see the code map):
- Wormholes (`wormholes`, Heap Space): a pair of mouths that skip a stretch of road, open on a fixed cycle. Drive in while open and you come out of the exit at the same speed; dive in as it collapses and you're spat out spinning (a segfault). The call: floor it to make the window, lift to arrive as it opens, or go the long way. Bots time it with an arrival estimate and wait when the wait costs less than the skip saves.
- Low gravity (`lowg`, Heap Space): a ramp that flies 2.2× as long over a crater, with a bend past the landing. A speed window: too slow drops you in the crater (restart well back), too fast sails you past the bend into the run-off. The window (`vLo`/`vHi`) is worked out when the track is built; bots aim inside it. Moonshot's Causeway Leap (rev 8, 2026-10-22, the user: "the jump at the beginning's green is sooo small"): the window was 276-290 (14 u/s: landing 40 past the gap, 22 inside the road's edge), and it lied both ways: Octo alone landed clean from 242, ran off at the bend from 284, and flat out (430) was as quick as the best clean landing, because past the bend was only dust. Now the hard left past the landing is 18 further on (`[2133,2725]`, the straight after the gap longer), the window is where you land past the gap (`clear` 4) inside the road's middle half (`edge` 36: where you still make the bend): 246-294 (48 u/s, the gauge's bar 60 either side, so the green is about 30% of it, was 7%), and landing past the road's edge is a crash landing in the scrub (`crash`: `CRASH_KEEP` 0.4 of your speed, a `CRASH_SPIN` 0.6 s spin). Measured (one kart alone, L1, no items, no timed obstacles, held at v to the lip, 500 before it to 700 past, s): Octo: splash at 236 and under; clean 242-302, 4.72 at 242 down to 4.20 at 302 (the top of the window is quickest); landing wide 308-326, 4.67-4.87 (a run through the dust at the bend); a crash 340-430, 5.48-5.83. Cpp (no grip) clean 236-284 (4.68-4.97), wide 290-326, a crash from 340 (5.23-5.67). Bots aim 30-60% up the window (`lowgAI`), and now get down to it `early` 90 short of the lip (lifting slows you less as you slow: planning to lift right up to the lip, they arrived 20-40 too fast), keep their boosts and leave the gantry arms' timing (race-timed.js `timedAI`) until after the landing (`c.ai.leapNear`: timing the arms from before the lip slowed them into the lagoon). In races (4 karts, items, 6 a level; L0 / L1 / L2): landed on the road 24/26, 23/25, 22/25 (clean 15, 16, 16; the rest a run through the dust at the bend), a crash 0, 1, 2, a splash 2, 1, 1 (mostly karts hit just before the lip); before (the old window, the same harness): on the road 13/30, 16/30, 10/32, off it 10, 8, 14, a splash 7, 3, 4.
- Stamping presses (`presses`, Assembly Line): heads that slam across the whole road on a beat, phased as a green wave for one speed. Under one when it lands: stopped dead and squashed out of it. Bots simulate their approach and pick the fastest speed (or a stop) that passes clean.
- Waves (`waves`, Merge Bay, js/race-waves.js): now and then a breaker rolls off the sea across a straight (from the side facing away from the track's middle). A foam line builds for 1.6 s, then the crest crosses the road in 0.7 s and shoves anyone it reaches toward land (and slows them). Lift to let it break ahead, or floor it to get through. Bots: through before it breaks or arrive after (eta), once it's foaming.
- Volcano (`volcano`, Duke's Volcano and Kernel Pretzel, where it stands inside the Caldera and erupts every 10-14 s): a cone beside the road rumbles, then erupts every 8-12 s (race.rng; `every` per volcano) and throws 2-4 globs of lava onto the road within its `reach`, 65% aimed just ahead of a kart (60% of those at a human if one is in reach). An eruption's globs never land on each other: a glob that would slides up or down the road (one way, past each one) until they're `SPACE` (1.15) × their sizes apart (a glob is about as wide as the road, so before this the ones aimed at the same kart often stacked into one spot). Each glob's shape is 16 race.rng draws at the throw (`globShape`: a radius every 9° round the impact point: lumps, a stretch along the throw, tendrils); that outline IS the hazard (`onGlob` tests the car's box against it) and what's drawn (target mark, the glob in flight, the pool). Under it when it lands, or driving into a pool's molten middle (`molten`: all of it for 0.45 s, then shrinking, crusted over at `CRUST` ~1.1 s; the crust is gone by 5 s; a melt-down sinks for 2.5 s and comes back 360 back, so waiting is always cheaper than driving in): you melt down (`c.lavaT`, an early return in `updateCar` like the moon) and come back up 360 back at a standstill. The crust round it only drags you while hot. The call: lift and let it land and crust over (the steering line goes round the molten part once there's room), or floor it past. Bots (`volcanoAI`) beat it if `eta` says they clear it before it lands (or it's too late to stop), else wait to arrive once the molten middle has shrunk enough to pass, and stop short of it meanwhile. Bots melt down about 0.2-0.3 times a race (L1/L2), L0 0.2 (Kernel Pretzel) to 0.8 (Duke's Volcano), 2026-10-18, 24 seeds; a bot notices each glob on its own roll, so spread-out globs catch the weak ones more. Drawing (`puddle` in draw-mechanics.js): the molten part at exactly the tested size, bright and churning with a glowing rim, bubbles, embers and the road's glow; the crust dark with dull seams; a puff of steam the moment it crusts over; a bot that fires a boost while waiting at a pool can still launch itself in (the boost items' `wantUse` doesn't know about lava).
- Lava river (`river`, Duke's Volcano, js/race-river.js): a channel from the crater's lip down the cone (`from`: the angle it leaves at) and the flank, across the road at two fords (`fords`: how many crossings from the top are fords; the rest are bridges, here the start straight) and into a lava lake (`lake`). When the volcano erupts, 55% of the time (`SURGE_P`, race.rng) lava spills into it and a surge runs down at `FLOW_V` (320/s), reaching the upper ford ~3.1 s after the eruption and the lower one ~1.5 s later, and floods each for `FLOOD` (1.3 s); on a ford then and you melt down as in a lava pool (`c.lavaT`, lavaSink). The ford's striped posts flash from 3.2 s before the front gets there (and a `warn` chip for a player). The call: cross before the front, or lift and let it pass. The two fords ask differently: the upper one is right out of the Funnel's hairpin (slow: you can't beat it from far, but waiting costs little), the lower one on a flat-out straight (easy to beat if you're early, costly to wait for). Bots (`riverAI`) simulate holding a speed (with the corners' limits) or holding it until the surge has passed and then flat out, and take the fastest that's clean by a skill margin, stopping `WAIT` short of the ford; they queue behind a kart waiting there (a bump from behind would shove it in) and miss a surge now and then (3-20% by skill).
- Long split (`forks` with `split: true`, Kernel Pretzel; `routes` route 0 is the outside road, route 1 the inside one): two very different roads for nearly half the lap that take about as long (the Big Loop and the Caldera, round the volcano). Over the last `SPLIT_LANE` (560) before the commit point the road is two lanes and the line moves into one by speed (`Race.splitLane`): the inside road's if you're under 1.3× its first bend's speed by your own grip (8% hysteresis), else the outside one's; at `sLock` (where the inside road is 0.9 half-widths in, the tip of the painted gore) you take the road whose lane you're in. `splitGuess` drives the dotted line (both roads, bright on the one you're set for) and the minimap. Progress on the inside road counts as the same share of the outside road's stretch (`R.split`, `progressOf`), not distance left. Bots pick with `splitOdds` (race-ai.js: about even, better and trailing bots in, leaders and a volcano about to blow out) and keep to their lane (`c.ai.tuckIn`). No item boxes at the split (`R.keepClear`). The art is `splitArt` (art/track.js); kerbs and edge lines aren't painted across the other road's lane at any fork. A course route only takes the `widths`, `jumps` and `gaps` on its own road. The volcano works on a course: a glob keeps where it is on every road it touches (`o.on[k]`), random ones land on any road in reach (`v.spots`). Measured (2026-10-19, rev 2, 48 races a level, 4 bots with items, split to rejoin): Big Loop 22.2 / 21.15 / 20.3 s (L0/L1/L2), the Caldera's long way 21.6 / 21.3 / 20.9 s, the Ash Run 20.9 / 20.2 / 20.1 s; bots take the Caldera 54-71% of laps (more the better they are) and the Ash Run on about a third of those.
- Slag pour (`slag`, Assembly Line, js/race-slag.js): the Crucible is a wide 180° (road 280 wide, twice the track's width, centreline radius 290) round a pot, with a ladle on a crane that tips a charge in on a beat (`period` [7, 7, 8.5] s, repeating: a longer wait every third pour so a lap doesn't keep meeting it at the same moment; `slagPhase`: tipping 1.4 s, the surge runs out across the inside `cover` 0.6 of the road in 0.45 s, holds 1.5 s, drains in 1.3 s and leaves a cooling crust, which is only for show). Caught by the molten slag: scorched once a pour (speed x0.4, a spin, thrown out to the dry side, `fxHit` 'slag'; costs about 1.5-2.4 s). The call is a big throttle difference: dry, lift hard (to ~260-300) and the line hugs the pot's rim; pouring while you're round the apex, keep it pinned (~340-400 by kart) and the line rides the far wall (`wide` 1), too slow and it tucks into the slag, too fast and you run off the outside; or lift and arrive just as it drains. The wide lane costs 0.6-0.95 s against the dry apex (measured solo, with a 20-40 wide speed window); before this rework it was one speed step on the road's centreline and the two answers were the same dab of the button (the user: too small, barely changed how you drive). The wide line sets up on the outside down the approach (`SETUP` 350 before the corner) and comes back in over the end of the corner, where the slag tapers, so it's not on the outer edge as the road narrows. The line keeps you out of slag that's already down (`slagLine`), but it doesn't see the surge coming. Bots decide as they turn in (`c.ai.slag`, locked from 120 before the corner): `apexMeets` checks the apex line against the slag at about apex speed; if it meets it they go wide (a third of the way into their speed window, `stayWide`), or wait at the entry if it drains within 0.2-0.4 s. No-item bot races: about 1 scorch in 200 passes. A pure function of the race clock, so no race state. Drawing: textures (molten metal with floating slag rafts, crust plates) made once into offscreen canvases and used as patterns turning round the crucible; a glow sprite stamped with 'lighter'; the crane and ladle in the over-cars layer.
- Hurricanes (`hurricane`, Async Atoll, js/race-hurricane.js, art in js/art/hurricane.js; replaced the squalls 2026-10-18, the user: "a hurricane that moves through the map"): every so often a storm tracks across the whole map on a gently curving path (enters off-map, crosses near the middle any way, exits; the first a few seconds after GO, each next one 7-16 s after the last has gone; about two a race). Per race `race.storms`: a few numbers per storm (`mx, my` the path's middle, `a` its heading there, `k` bend per 1000 units, `v` speed, `rm` eyewall radius 375-475, `tm` when it's at the middle, `h` half its crossing time), drawn from `race.rng` in `hurricaneUpdate` only as the last one nears its middle, rounded to 3 decimals so the host and every screen work out the same storm (`stormAt`, `windAt`: no other state; a snapshot carries ~200 bytes for them only when one is added or in a full refresh). The wind (`windAt`): round the eye anticlockwise on screen and `INFLOW` inward, 0 in the eye (`EYE` 0.45 rm), up to `PEAK` (1.5) in the eyewall, falling as (rm/r)^0.7 out to `OUT` (3.2) rm, a little stronger on the side blowing the way the storm moves. On the road it's scaled by `exposure` (the open causeway 1, the islands `LAND` 0.85) and the part across by `crossShelter` (none on the banked seawall, so the banking is untouched). Effect as the squalls had: the part along the road is `TAIL`·along·v extra ground speed, the part across shoves you at side·|side|·v²/(2·hold); the steering pushes back at most half your speed, so past that you're pressed to the edge and scrape (a knock, speed bleeding off, sparks, `scrape`): on the causeway the breakwaters always hold you on the tarmac, elsewhere only the wind's own push is stopped at the kerb (you can still drive off). No falling in. Fair warning: the `thunder` cue and a `storm` sign for everyone as each storm reaches the atoll, a wind sign (`cross`/`tail`/`head`) for a player when a strong wind is on the road 1-2.5 s ahead, the storm on the minimap (`miniStorms`: its eyewall ring, a soft disc over the strong wind, and a dashed trail with an arrowhead where it's heading), the clouds and their shadow sweeping in. The call: ride a tailwind flat out; in a crosswind lift a touch or carry the speed and scrape (measured about even: alone at L2, holding the bots' caps 96.33 s vs ignoring the wind 96.10 s with 0.4 s of scraping, calm 93.86 s); the eye is calm. Bots (`hurricaneAI`) cap their speed for the strongest crosswind they'll meet in the next ~1000 units (where the storm will be by then; a skill-dependent share of hold/side²), and keep boosts for later when a strong crosswind or the eyewall is within 1.5 s (`c.gustNear`, `straightAhead`). Measured (48 bot races with items, L0-L2): winner +0.6 s over a calm race (the squalls were +1.6 s), no extra crawling, scraping 0.02% of kart-time. Drawing (art/hurricane.js): two cached cloud sprites per storm turning round the eye at different speeds (eyewall faster), translucent over the cars, their shadow under the cars, a sunlit eye; the eyewall a ring with a bright rim round the eye that falls off quickly outside, its shadow darker than the cloud (the shadow is under the karts, the cloud over them; 2026-10-19, the user: slightly easier to see); whitecaps, wind streaks, rain, debris and the palms (drawn live from `atollLand(T).onLand.palms`, bending with the local wind) from the clock and `windAt`; pennants and windsocks on the breakwaters.
- The giant crab (`crab`, Crab Harbor, js/race-crab.js): the Borrow Checker scuttles sideways along the beach beside a stretch of road, keeping pace with the karts coming. Every few seconds (rests `every`, race.rng) it picks a kart in reach (a human 60% of the time) and rears a claw over where that kart will be in 1.3 s (`WIND`; aimed at 0.6-1.15 of that: a short one you can beat flat out, a long one you lift for; held flat through every pass a kart is hit about half the time): the claw's shadow darkens on the road between two flashing red bars, then it slams right across the road and lies there 0.5 s. Under it, or driving into it before it has lifted off (`HURT`), and you're swatted toward land: speed × 0.42 and a 0.5 s spin (a knock, not a wreck). From the targeted kart's lap 2 it's a pincer: the front claw at the aim, the back one 400 behind it 0.45 s later, so the way through is lift as it rears and go as the first claw lands. Bots (`crabAI`, run after `pressAI`) simulate holding a speed, or holding it until the first slam and then flat out, and take the fastest that stays out from under every claw they've seen (a worse bot misses one now and then); `c.crabNear` keeps their boosts off. Bots are hit about 0.2-0.4 times a race. Its state (`race.crab`: where it is, its strikes with their slam times, who's been hit) is plain data, so it goes online as is. Drawing: the beach, burrow, loot and warning signs (static), its shadow, the claw shadows and red bars (under the cars), the crab, its arms, claws and eyes on stalks looking at its target (over the cars); it scowls on the last lap.
- Jackpots (`jackpots`, Pharaoh's Jackpot, js/race-jackpot.js): three golden boxes along the inside of a corner's apex, 16 in from the kerb (6 was too hard to reach) (gilded kerb, an Eye of Horus painted on the road). Lifting tucks your line in onto them; on the gas you pass in the middle. A golden box (touched within 26, not 32, and not pulled toward like plain boxes) spins a slot machine over the kart for 1.05 s (`c.slot`, plain data: online-safe), outcome drawn from `car.rng` at the pickup: bust 20% (a cobra: 0.2 s stall), win 45% (two alike: an item + 0.7 s coin boost), jackpot 35% (three alike: an item in every free hand + 1.6 s boost). No other box counts while it spins. Six on Pharaoh's (chicane kinks 2 and 4, the end of Jackpot Avenue, the Spike's exit, the bottom of the Staircase and its third step; the Gate hairpin, the Carousel and the two R240 lefts were tried and give no call: the line is on the kerb anyway, or lifting doesn't cost). Measured (one kart, no boxes, items off, 24 runs): tucking in costs 0.22 / 0.50 / 0.05 / 0.55 / 0.41 / 0.29 s, 0.34 on average, the speed payouts give back about 0.26 s a spin, so with the items it's a fair bet. Items per kart per lap (4 bots): 7.7, 5.0 of them from the Strip and 2.7 from golden boxes (it was 14.75 with 12 rows round the lap; a normal track is about 3.2). Bots decide per corner and lap (`c.ai.jack`): 0.1 + (skill - 0.92) * 1.6 + 0.5 * how far back + 0.15 with both hands free, and cap their speed so they're tucked in by the middle box (75-85% reach a box when going for it, under 10% when not). Per-track boxes: `boxRows` (rows per lap), `boxLanes` (where across the road each box of a row sits) and `boxZone` (`[[x, y], [x, y]]`: the rows go evenly along that stretch only, `boxZoneOf` in geometry.js; the Strip's bazaar stalls are drawn from it by `art/pharaoh.js`, hooked on the first jackpot's static drawing) in the definition; other tracks keep their two-box rows.
- Banked corners (`banks`, Async Atoll): the outer half of a long corner tilts up, so grip rises toward the top lane (up to 1 + `bank`), and the line rides as high as your speed needs. That line is one smooth arc: a lane per car (`c.bankL`, slope `c.bankSl`) that slides across the banking no faster than the kart can follow (stopping a climb takes the grip to spare, so it eases up the wall from before the corner, where the banking's grip already fades in, `BANK_IN`); never set the steering target straight from the speed there, or karts slalom up and down the wall. The opposite of everywhere else: arriving hot is rewarded on the outside. Too hot even for the top and you go over (with `sea`, into the water); lift and you drop to the flat apex, slower. Bots ride at a skill-dependent height (a speed zone in the plan). Moonshot's crater bowl (Kepler) is a banked corner too, round 270° on a widened road, with the Moon's grip (`grip`) and `bowl` for its art (see Crater bowls below).
- Boulder run (`boulders`, Ruby Ruins, js/race-boulders.js): a stone serpent's head straddles the top of the longest straight (you drive in under it and out of its jaws). At irregular intervals (6-10 s, a timetable per race in `race.rocks` from `race.rng`; positions are worked out from the release times, nothing else is kept) its ruby eyes glow for 2.2 s (`WARN`) and it drops a boulder out of its jaws. The boulder fills the road and rolls down it (from 140, gathering speed to 760, far faster than a kart), can't make the hairpin at the bottom and rolls on into the gorge. In front of it when it gets you: flattened (held still for 1.6 s, then crawling off). Behind it (ran into it, or it dropped just ahead): held at its pace. The call when the eyes light up: floor it if you'll be well clear of the jaws before it drops (about 1.4 s), or lift and let it go first. Measured solo (L2, no items): waiting as the bots do costs about 0.6 s a race against no boulders, never lifting about 0.9 s (0.4 flattenings a race). Bots (`boulderAI`) simulate their run down to the hairpin (`runTimes`), and if a boulder would catch them they wait at a line short of the jaws (crawling up to arrive just after it's out); they follow one at a gap, and keep boost items while waiting or with one about to drop (`rockShy`).
- Logic gate (`gates` plus a fork with `gate: true`, Silicon Circuit, js/race-gate.js): a push switch on the inside kerb at the exit of a corner (the Switch), and a fork 3.6-3.8 s further on (the Logic Gate) whose inside road (the Bus, route 1) runs through a gate. The gate is a one-shot timer on the race (`race.gate`: `until`, `at`, `by`, plain data): drive over the switch (within `SW_R` 36 of it, so you have to tuck right in: lifting puts you there) and it opens for `open` (5.2 s) for anyone, you and whoever's behind you; pressing again while it's open restarts it. In the last `CLOSE` (0.6 s) the bars slide shut, and a kart crossing them on the Bus then (not a Ghost, not a Firewall: it smashes through) is thrown back onto the long way at its inside edge with 20% of its speed and a 0.8 s spin. Fully shut, a player's line stays out of the apex (`gateLine`), so you go round. A kart that runs wide off one road onto the other where they split is put on that road (never onto the Bus past the bars). The call: press (it costs ~0.1-0.2 s) and floor it to the gate, or ride someone else's window; at the fork, lift into the Bus if you'll beat the bars, else stay on the gas and go the long way. Measured (one kart, no items, L2/L0): the Bus is 1.1-1.3 s a lap quicker than the long way, pressing costs 0.10-0.13 s a lap, hitting the bars costs 3.9 s against making it (2.9 s against going round), so diving in pays only when you're about 75% sure. Bots (`gateAI`): at the fork they go in if `eta` says the gate will still be open with a skill-dependent margin (committed once tucked in), else `stayWide`; at the switch they press when the gate won't be open for them anyway, with a chance by skill and place (0.45 + (skill - 0.92) * 2.5 + 0.3 * how far back). In 8-kart races with items about 70% of laps take the Bus (more at L0/L1, where the pack stays together and rides each other's presses) and a bot hits the bars about once in 12 races. `gateGuess` (via `Race.splitGuess`) points the dotted line and the minimap down the Bus while it's open and you're slow enough to tuck in. Drawing: the switch, its copper trace to the gate (lit like a burning fuse while it's open, its dark end creeping to the bars over the open time: stay ahead of it and you're in time), the transistors and their LED countdown, the bars, the chevrons or red cross on the inside lane and an AND gate painted on the Bus (`gateStatic`, `gateUnder` in draw-mechanics.js).

### Building one
1. Add the definition to `TRACKS` (name, `desc`, `width`, `scale`, `theme`, `pts`, plus the mechanic keys). A new mechanic usually needs: data in the definition, setup in `buildTrack` (`js/geometry.js`), the rule in the race update (`js/race.js` or its own `js/race-*.js`), what the bots do about it (`js/race-ai.js`), and drawing (`js/art/` / `js/render.js`; a new theme `deco` gets a `LOOK` entry, props and a landmark).
2. Teach the bots the mechanic. A mechanic the AI ignores makes the track's balance numbers meaningless.
3. `node test/sim.test.mjs`: it fails on the new track with "no golden". Check every car finishes, then `--update`.
4. Watch it: `index.html#demo=N`. Look for corners where the bots all run off, or all crawl.
5. Add it to `README.md` under Tracks.
