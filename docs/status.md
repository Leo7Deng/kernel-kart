- Fight feels like a real fight (2026-10-23, the user: "the fighting mode isnt especially fun for me, the fighting doesnt feel super responsive, and kinda 1 dimensional just fast hit or block"; "idk if i especially like the smash style shield + grab, because its almost like you need insanely fast reaction time to see when theyre shielding, and then grab"; "no more smash like perfect frames and etc, i want it more real fighting. yes shield just in front is good for now. think of other ways to get it out of this smash style."). Why it felt that way: only the light chain buffered a press (a heavy, special, guard or grab pressed a little early was lost), the dodge fired on guard's release, walking took 0.2 s to get going (Tux 0.7 s, 2 s to stop), a heavy started on release, attacks went wherever you already faced, a jab was 2-3 % of health so a round was ~20 small hits, and the answer to a shield was a grab within 3 frames of seeing it: a reaction test. Plus the Smash-isms: perfect blocks, launches with DI and an air move, knockback growing as health dropped, generic grabs and throws, dodge invincibility. Now (js/fight.js's header, fight-chars.js, docs/fight-frames.md): every press is buffered 8 frames and fires on the first free frame; walking is near-instant (full speed in ~4 frames), backing away slower; a heavy winds up on the press and charges while held (0.8 s to full, which knocks down); a move aims at the foe (or the held way within 60°) as it starts and keeps its facing, so whiffs are real. The shield covers your front only (70°, Ferris 100°): blocking you walk slowly and turn slowly, a blocked hit chips 10 % and costs stamina, a hit from round the side lands. Stamina pays for every attack, step, whiff and blocked hit; empty, you're gassed (slower attacks and walk, and a blocked hit staggers you open): that's how a turtle loses, visibly, with no reaction test. Clean hits matter: a counter hit (in their wind-up) x1.4, from the side x1.2, from behind x1.4. Guard + a direction is a step (no invincibility; a sideways step circles them, a forward one turns a light or heavy into a lunge). Knockdowns instead of launches (a full charge, a finisher on a counter or flank, a wall slam, the big kit hits), untouchable while down and getting up. Bigger, fewer hits (health 100-122, lights ~5-6, heavies ~15-20), a landed light cancels into heavy or special, two swings that meet both land (a trade). No grab, no throws, no perfect block, no DI, no air moves: the grab key is gone; Pyth's Coil keeps its hold (unblockable, step out of it); the supers that launched knock down; Swifty's two air moves became quicker get-ups and stamina, Pyth's dodge longer steps, Ferris's shield floor a wider guard and cheaper blocks, Tux slides on after a step. Bots (built on the human-speed perception that landed just before, js/fight-ai.js): block front-on and turn into someone stepping round them, guard up in reach as a decision (jabs are too quick to see), jab into a slow wind-up they've seen (a counter hit) or step out of it, punish whiffs, confirm a landed light into the heavy, step round a turtle or press it until it gasses, mind their own stamina and push a tired foe, wait just outside reach and go in now and then, stay off walls. Numbers (bot v bot on the Sky Ring): at Normal every fighter 43-55 % (60 fights a pair: Tux 52, Duke 54, Gopher 45, Ferris 50, Pyth 54, Octo 48, Docky 50, Swifty 50, Cpp 43, Bugdroid 51), 6 of 45 pairs outside 35-65 % (the worst Duke 75-25 Docky and 72-28 Ferris: the zoner against the slow), rounds a median 24 s (73 % in 20-45 s), the round's winner landing a median 14 clean hits; per round about 25 hits, 11 blocks, 2 counter hits, 3 flanks, 2 knockdowns, 7 steps, 1 gassing. Chill rounds run a median 35 s, Ruthless 19 s (the better bots punish and confirm more; their pairs spread wider, 10-13 of 45 outside at 30 a pair). Levels: Normal beats Chill 86 %, Ruthless beats Normal 82 %, Ruthless beats Chill 98 % (test/fight-levels.mjs). Tux v Duke 43 / 53 / 69 % at Chill / Normal / Ruthless (200 fights a level). Learned tuning it: bots first brawled inside both reaches and every round was over in 10-15 s; they needed footsies (wait outside reach, commit in bursts) and a decided guard. Stamina only bit once it cost more (0.08 a light) and regrew slower (0.3/s). A hop or dive cut short left a fighter hanging in the air where nothing could reach them (a stalemate in sudden death): anyone not in a move that lifts them now drops back to the floor. Reach is still the most sensitive number (one unit of Duke's jab moved Tux v Duke 10 points). Hard wall slams are rare now (about 0.1 a round, they were 1-1.6): hits knock back less than launches did.
- Fight bots react at human speed (2026-10-23, the user: "i feel like the bot difficulties react way faster than a human"). Before, the bots read the foe's live state: a decision every `react` 0.32 / 0.18 / 0.1 s (Chill / Normal / Ruthless), and the perfect block, the punish and DI came on the same step as the cue (a bot blocked a charge up close a median 0.18 / 0.18 / 0.07 s after it started, and Normal perfect-blocked about 5 hits a round, jabs with 0.05 s startups among them). Now (js/fight-ai.js `perceive`) a bot sees what the foe was doing 0.42 / 0.32 / 0.24 s ago (plus up to 0.14 / 0.1 / 0.08, re-rolled each think) and where it is 0.14 / 0.12 / 0.1 s ago carried on by its walk; its own state it knows at once; DI and the air move wait until the launch is felt. So jabs, tapped heavies, dives, zaps and tentacles are too quick to answer on sight: the bots answer them on reads (guarding up close to a foe who's been swinging, or in reach of a ready, aimed special they've been using; Ferris's Borrow Checker up before a swing or into a string), and a grab or waiting beats a read. A whiff is punished off the swing seen and its frame data; perfect blocks only on slow, telegraphed moves. The charge reaction is now a median 0.4-0.57 s (the quick tail is guess blocks). Ruthless keeps its edge on decisions: it still beats Normal 82 % of fights and Normal beats Chill 87 % (before: 81 % and 99 %; `node test/fight-levels.mjs`, every fighter v every fighter). The balance moved, because the kits that rely on reacting (blocking or dodging a special, countering) lost most and the quick specials gained: at Normal on the ring (20 fights a pair) Tux 60, Duke 38, Gopher 51, Ferris 32, Pyth 64, Octo 52, Docky 46, Swifty 50, Cpp 46, Bugdroid 62 % (before 46-54 % each); Tux v Duke 77 / 74 / 60 % at Chill / Normal / Ruthless (was 54 / 55 / 47). Tuning tried in the bot: a higher block chance, more guessing and a slower position track didn't help Duke or Ferris much (the samples are noisy, ±8 points at 16 a pair). Left for the fighters' numbers (js/fight-chars.js): Ferris (24-32 %), Duke (~38 %), Bugdroid (62-71 %), Ruthless Gopher (~77 %).

- Fight combat system (2026-10-23, the user asked to "think about the balance of some moves, and priority and shield and if things should be able to be hit out of their move/animation"; on the shield: "you should always be able to shield, but the damage the shields can absorb go down, and slowly regenerate, you should know from the shield getting smaller at lower healths"; on effects: "make everything have good looking effects, doesnt have to be over the top, and certainly not just circle or ring of colors"). Moves have startup (hit = cancelled), active (trades by priority: light 1, heavy/special 2, super 3; higher wins, equal = a clash: both bounce apart, a spark, no damage) and recovery (punishable). Armor per move: light armor soaks light hits in startup (damage taken, no flinch: Tux's Belly Flop wind-up, any fully charged heavy), super armor on supers' wind-up soaks everything but grabs. The shield is a pool: blocked hits drain it (heavies more), a growing share leaks through as it drains (none at full, 85 % empty), it regrows slowly off guard, never breaks (the guard break and its dizzy are gone), grabs ignore it; block stun scaled by the hit, attacker pushback, a blocked contact move goes straight into its recovery (heavies and specials unsafe on block), a perfect block (guard ≤ 0.1 s before the hit) takes nothing and staggers the attacker 0.4 s. Stale moves (−10 % per repeat among your last 5 hits), hitstun ×(1 − 0.13 per hit in the combo, ≥ 0.45). Dodge 0.3 s, invincible for the first 0.16. Per-move data with defaults by kind (fight-chars.js header; `docs/fight-frames.md`, `node test/fight-frames.mjs`), so the 8 new fighters only write data. Bots punish moves stuck in recovery, don't jab into armor (grab it or wait), time perfect blocks (Normal and Ruthless 25 %; a hit already inside the window is too late for a bot that didn't), dodge heavies on a worn shield. Effects: a clash is a white-hot four-point glint with metal sparks flung across the line; a perfect block flares the bubble with a sweep of light and a crisp cold glint; armor soaking a hit runs a sheen of light down the model (pale steel, gold for super armor); the shield is a soap-film bubble lit from the top left, sized by its pool (eased), cooling from cyan to amber and cracking as it wears, dented by each blocked hit; sounds for each (a metal ting, a rising chime, a dull thud). Rebalanced: the new rules leaned on Duke (his many small hits go stale, his jabs lose to Tux's flop armor and toboggan priority), so Duke's health 98 → 110 and his headbutt's recovery 0.32 → 0.38 (unsafe on block); Chill bots block more (0.15 → 0.35) and mind walls more, punish less.
